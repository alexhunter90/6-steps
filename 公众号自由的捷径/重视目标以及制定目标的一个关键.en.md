# Valuing Goals, and One Key to Setting Them

*Original: [重视目标以及制定目标的一个关键](重视目标以及制定目标的一个关键.md) · English translation by Claude (Anthropic, Opus 5.5), November 2026 · [Back to English contents](../README.md)*

*Words said in English in the original are kept as they were said and shown in italics.*

---

25 September 2021 · The Shortcut to Freedom

## Conversation 1 — 20 November 2020

**Question:** I'd like to ask your advice.

**Question:** How can I move towards freedom faster?

**Feng:** Use it constantly. Use it to the utmost.

**Feng:** I can't give better advice than that.

**Feng:** I strongly recommend you use it on everything.

**Feng:** Make use of those goals.

**Feng:** Use constant gaining to spur yourself to release,

**Feng:** to boost your momentum in releasing.

**Feng:** If you get into the habit of using it on every one of your goals, you'll soon get to that place.

---

## Conversation 2 — 2 January 2021

**Feng:** Value goals.

**Feng:** In theory, you can take freedom directly as your goal.

**Feng:** But I know that most people who say they want freedom are suppressing their longing for the world.

**Feng:** Why did Lester like the fourth pointer best? Because it means the most to people.

**Feng:** It's the most practical.

**Question:** You took freedom directly as your goal, didn't you?

**Feng:** I did, but I also used that teaching.

**Feng:** I wrote down all the things I wanted.

**Question:** I combine them too.

**Feng:** The first few pointers often lose their practicality and become mere theory.

**Question:** I can't do the first few yet. I'd better get everything I want first.

**Question:** Becoming a success is still necessary.

**Question:** I'm using the first few pointers too — getting all happiness from within, facing the fear of death directly. I'm using them all.

**Feng:** So far, everyone I've seen who claims to want freedom more than the world has turned it into mere theory.

**Question:** In this passage Lester explains why he liked *get everything you want by releasing only* best:

> **The Way 4** — **Student:** When you say it's your favourite, you mean…
> **Lester:** The one I use most.
> **Student:** I think my favourite is getting everything I want by releasing.
> **Lester:** That's my favourite too.
> **Student:** Does that mean it's more positive?
> **Lester:** Because you can see that this is the action you need to take to go free. The most direct action towards freedom is releasing all the obstacles. So today we're going to do Releasing 102 — the first time was Releasing 101. If you've been to college, you'll know what I mean. *(laughter)* I don't know what your colleges are like. *(laughter)* In my day, the first course was 101 and the second was 102. So today is Releasing 102, which means everyone has to release 102 times, and I'll count it towards your credits. *(laughter)* I suggest you make whatever statements will remind you: “Get everything through releasing.” They're all in your room, those signs. This is the most efficient way to go free. Make sure you go free. Now, is this working for all of you? Getting things without effort? Effort steadily decreasing? Have you ever got things effortlessly? With no effort at all — just falling into your lap?

**Feng:**

> So what do you need to do? In the previous four days of the course I summed it up. The first is that you want freedom more than you want the world. Following from that, you should get all your happiness from yourself — through releasing, from within. That's lesson 1. Lesson 2 is: you must make the decision to be free, and then you do it. And if you make the decision to be free, you will do it. It's that simple. If you're not free, then you haven't made the decision to be free. Because once you make the decision, everything you do afterwards will be towards freedom. The most basic thing is releasing constantly. The third lesson is to face the fear of dying directly and release it. Release the fear of dying directly. As you know, underneath approval and control is the fear of dying. So get rid of this driver at the very bottom, and you're finished. The fourth is: from now on, get every single thing you want through releasing. I'd guess this is the most practical of the four. How successful have you been? Are you still doing things through the eyes of the little ego most of the time? Remember, when we do things through releasing, it's effortless. If you're putting in effort, that's ego-driven.

> **Translator's note:** Both quoted passages are Lester's words from *The Way*, translated here from Feng's Chinese, not Lester's original English. They match the translations in [Lester's Favourite, Most Practical Teaching](莱斯特最喜欢最实践性的教导：仅仅通过释放获得一切.en.md).

**Feng:** Practicality — this is very important.

**Feng:** Why did Lester say the fourth is the most practical? Because you often can't get the earlier ones working.

## Conversation 3 — 30 December 2020

**Feng:** You need to set a goal you really want.

**Feng:** You need motivation.

**Question:** Is a million what I really want?

**Feng:** Do you feel that excitement?

**Question:** But once I'm free, I'll have everything.

**Question:** So why don't I go straight for freedom?

**Feng:** That's fine too.

**Feng:** Taking freedom directly as your goal is fine too.

**Question:** Do you have any suggestions?

**Feng:** You can write down everything you want.

---

## Conversation 4 — 2 January 2021

**Feng:** Dare to dream.

**Question:** Could I become a billionaire through releasing too?

**Feng:** Of course.

**Feng:** A couple of days ago, someone in the group also felt he didn't want any goals —

**Feng:** until I suggested one to him:

**Feng:** go to the Eastman School of Music, the best in the world, find a blonde girl and live the romantic life he loves most.

**Feng:** He lit up instantly.

**Feng:** *(sniggering emoji)*

**Question:** *(three laughing emojis)*

**Feng:** So dare to dream.

**Feng:** The reason many people feel they don't want the world is simply that they don't dare to dream — they've suppressed it.

**Feng:** They think it's impossible, so they don't want the world.

**Feng:** That isn't really wanting freedom.

**Question:** I've found that I was suppressing it. I thought it was impossible and didn't dare to dream.

**Feng:** *dare think big*

**Feng:** Lester said that.

**Feng:** Dare to think big.

**Question:** But so far I haven't achieved even one small goal.

**Question:** In that situation, can I go straight to thinking big?

**Feng:** If it's not a goal you really want, you'll have no motivation.

**Feng:** One purpose of a goal is to give you motivation to release.

**Feng:** Once that group member found a goal that excited him, his releasing sped up.

**Question:** So the key is to set what you really want.

---

## Conversation 5 — 8 January 2021

**Feng:** You can set any goal you want,

**Feng:** as long as it gives you motivation to keep releasing.

**Question:** So I don't have to practise on small goals first to build up momentum? As long as the goal helps me release constantly, it's meaningful for me — have I understood that right?

**Feng:** Mm. If that's what you want most, then use that.

**Question:** I understand. Thank you.

---

## Conversation 6 — 22 December 2020

**Question:** Teacher, can releasing heal a family member's illness? Or achieve other things — to do with money, career, relationships, or anything else at all?

**Feng:** For questions like “can it do such-and-such?”, I hope you'll all remember something Lester said:

**Feng:** “*every impossible, no matter how impossible, become immediately a possible ,as you completely released on it.*”

**Feng:** “Every impossible, no matter how impossible, immediately becomes a possible, once you have completely released on it.”
