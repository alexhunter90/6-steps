## Conversation 1

**Question:**
Teacher, you said that reaching *nirvikalpa samadhi* means completely releasing the karma of the physical world, and that you're actually already free at that point. At that stage there's still the karma of the astral body and the causal body left — are those free too?

**Feng:**
**You can reach *nirvikalpa samadhi* without even releasing the karma of the physical world. Your viewpoint has already shifted.**

**Question:**
**Releasing the karma of the physical world means you've reached *anāgāmī* (non-returner)?**

**Feng:**
Mm.

**Question:**
So reaching the first fruit is actually reaching *nirvikalpa samadhi*?

**Feng:**
Yes. A stream-entry noble one will never fall back.

**Question:**
That's why you said “you can reach *nirvikalpa samadhi* without even releasing the karma of the physical world.”

**Feng:**
Mm.

**Question:**
On reaching *anāgāmī*, the karma of the astral body is released, and the karma of the causal body is still left?

**Feng:**
The karma of both the astral body and the causal body is still left. An *anāgāmī* will not come back to the physical world. In Buddhist terms, he still retains craving for the realm of form and craving for the formless realm.

**Question:**
Where does an *anāgāmī* noble one go?

**Feng:**
An *anāgāmī* noble one goes to the Pure Abodes — what Yogananda called Hiranyaloka.

**Question:**
Why doesn't an *anāgāmī* noble one just go the whole way and reach the end?

**Feng:**

> “‘Why doesn't a master just dissolve all his karma,’ I asked, ‘when he is one with God?’
> ‘It's because, in that state, you simply don't care. You see that all of this is only a dream. You may even go on reincarnating, coming back to the world to help your disciples gain liberation. Masters at this level may even deliberately keep a tiny bit of karma, to tie themselves to this plane for a while, for that higher purpose. Once you have reached the highest state, *nirbikalpa samadhi*, ego-consciousness no longer exists. You are essentially already free.’
> ‘Master,’ I asked, ‘what is it that makes a soul come back to the world after gaining final liberation?’
> He replied, ‘He still keeps a “desireless desire” to help others.’”

> **Translator's note:** This appears to be from a conversation between Yogananda and his disciple (probably from Swami Kriyananda's *Conversations with Yogananda*). It is translated here from the Chinese; the original English isn't in the repository. The English terms in brackets in the Chinese (*nirbikalpa samadhi*, *ego-consciousness*, *desireless desire*) are kept.

It isn't necessary.

**Question:**
Mm. But after reaching ultimate freedom and becoming a *jivanmukta*, can't you also go on helping people? Like you and Lester.

**Feng:**
A *jivanmukta* (editor's note: meaning here **the fourth fruit, *arahant***) does not come back. Let me correct how I put that. Strictly speaking, *jivanmukta* is the Hindu tradition's general term covering everyone from the first fruit to the fourth, though it can usually refer to someone who has reached the end.

When you reach *arahant*, you no longer create new karma, so you necessarily attain *paramukta*, becoming a *siddha* and merging into the Infinite.

**Question:**
Strictly speaking, then, **are you now an *arahant*? Have you reached the fourth stage of *nirvikalpa samadhi*?** Have you completely released the karma of the physical, astral and causal bodies, while keeping some desireless desire in order to help others?

**Feng:**
Mm.

**Question:**
What does “will not come back” mean here?

**Feng:**
It means will not come back.

> “One who longs for the Ineffable (that is, nirvana),
> whose mind is filled with right thought (the third fruit),
> and who no longer clings to the realm of desire —
> such a one is called ‘one who goes upstream’.
>
> (Note: *uddhaṃsoto*, ‘one who goes upstream’, means one who flows upward — that is, an *anāgāmī* noble one who will certainly be born in the Pure Abodes.)”

That is a verse of the Buddha's from the *Dhammapada*. “One who goes upstream” means one who will never go downward again.

**Question:**
So, having reached the fourth fruit, *arahant*, you won't come back?

**Feng:**
When you reach *anāgāmī*, you won't come back to the physical world. When you reach *arahant*, you reach nirvana without remainder — what we usually call merging into the Infinite.

**Question:**
Mm. And having reached the third fruit, you already won't come back?

**Feng:**
You won't come back to the physical world; you'll only be in Hiranyaloka, until you reach *arahant*. The Pure Land of Bliss that later people invented is modelled on the Pure Abodes, but the Pure Abodes are the dwelling place of *anāgāmī* noble ones.

**Question:**
Does an *arahant* also go to Hiranyaloka?

**Feng:**
An *arahant* does not go to Hiranyaloka. An *arahant* merges directly into the Infinite when the physical body ends. Having reached the fourth fruit and attained perfection, you enter nirvana without remainder. If you come back after that, you are an avatar. An avatar is the descent of perfect infinite being.

**Question:**
So when Lester left the world, he merged directly into the Infinite?

**Feng:**
I told you that a long time ago.

**Question:**
Later I heard you say that after Lester left, many of his students saw him.

**Feng:**
Only in some form.

**Question:**
You also said that after merging into the Infinite, one doesn't appear in any form.

**Feng:**
Merging into infinite being isn't disappearing; it's existing without form.

**Question:**
Ralph said that the day after Lester left, Lester came to see him. In what form did Lester come?

**Feng:**
**In a dream. You can see Lester in any form. Lester is no longer a body. Lester is right beside every one of us now, in the “form” of infinite being.**

If you want to see Lester in astral form, you'll see him in astral form. If you want to see Lester in physical form, you'll see Lester in physical form. It depends on your acceptance.

But he is everywhere, at every point. There is nowhere he isn't present at this moment. After merging into the Infinite, he no longer appears in any form of his own.

**Question:**
If my acceptance were great enough, could I still see Lester in physical form?

**Feng:**
Mm. That isn't him “appearing” — he's here now. You just haven't projected his physical form. It isn't that a “Lester” turns up as a physical body; it's that you're trying to communicate with the body of a “Lester”.

If many people around the world projected Lester's body at the same time, they would all see Lester's body at once. But that wouldn't be Lester coming down; it would be the all-powerful working of infinite being.

**Question:**
After merging into the Infinite, Lester is no longer a body but exists in the form of “infinite being”. But if I wanted to see Lester in physical or astral form (and could accept it), I could still see Lester in physical form — is that right?

**Feng:**
Yes. Jesus and the Buddha too. Lester also told his students you could have coffee with Jesus.

**Question:**
Having merged into the Infinite, no longer a body (physical, astral or causal), no longer reincarnating — a *siddha* exists for ever at every point in the universe in the form of “infinite being”, and goes on helping others?

**Feng:**
Mm.

**Question:**
A *siddha* will still go on helping others gain freedom — only in the form of infinite being, not as a body?

**Feng:**
He unconsciously projects everyone, helping them gain freedom. In fact a *siddha's* help is the greatest of all; it is at the level of infinite being. Your mind just can't understand it, so you think teaching in words helps more. In reality, the formless help is the greatest.

**Question:**
The scope of a *siddha's* help is all life and all existence, while a *jivanmukta* only helps a few people?

**Feng:**
A *jivanmukta* also helps all life. The masters are all unconsciously projecting all life, even if they're sitting in a cave. But a *jivanmukta* hasn't finished playing the game of helping people; he still keeps some limitation. A *siddha* is a perfect being.

**Question:**
“A *jivanmukta* also helps all life” — which stage exactly is meant by *jivanmukta* here?

**Feng:**
The first fruit to the fourth.

**Question:**
A stream-entry noble one can help all life too?

**Feng:**
Mm. Not just a stream-entry noble one — **every time you release, you are helping all life.** **The world is only a projection of the mind.**

**Question:**
How does that help? For example, if I reached *nirvikalpa samadhi* but my mother doesn't study or practise, can it still help her?

**Feng:**
It helps far more than you sharing things with her would. People don't know how karma works. **Releasing makes you more selfless, and as long as you have a selfless consciousness, your mind will project a more perfect world.** That is real help.

But in an AGFLAP state, **so-called helping others is only gratifying the ego.** Feeding the ego doesn't produce much help in itself. Once you're free, even if you sit in a cave doing nothing, you help more than all the activists put together.

**Question:**
Every time I release, I certainly help my own world — and I can help my mother's world too?

**Feng:**
Yes. Every time you release, you help everyone.

**Question:**
Every time I release, I help everyone's world. Everyone means everyone?

**Feng:**
Everyone means everyone.

## Conversation 2

**Question:**
Teacher, how does karma work?

**Feng:**
**Karma is programs — action and reaction.** Programs create every one of your thoughts. Your thinking isn't free, so your actions aren't free either. Programs drive thoughts, and thoughts project the world. That's how it works.

**The world is only a display screen.** Whatever you do in the world is just smashing the screen; it can't change any of the story playing on it.

**Question:**
So releasing the programs directly can change the world?

**Feng:**
Yes. Rather, **only releasing the programs can change the world; otherwise the world will play out according to the programs already set.** Within the programs you have no free will — unless you decide to release the programs.

That's why, whatever schemes you've all wanted to try before, I've always said: go and release. Scheming is useless. It's just being driven by programs, repeating past karma, nothing more.

**Question:**
**Can I change my family's world through releasing? For example, I want to heal my mother's illness through releasing (even if she preset this illness herself).**

**Feng:**
Yes.

**Question:**
So I can change other people's karma through releasing?

**Feng:**
**You aren't changing anyone else's karma. This is the harmonious working of the universe.**

**Question:**
Isn't that interfering in someone else's life?

**Feng:**
At this point you're nowhere near interfering with karma. That's something said to people who are free, because **a free person can do anything and can go beyond all the bonds of karma.**

Right now you're being driven by karma. All your desires are part of karma, so all you affect is your own karma.

**That includes your mother's illness — that is your karma.** You're in no position to interfere with anyone else's karma. “Interfere” is a big word; you can't interfere with anyone before you're liberated.

**Question:**
My mother's illness is my karma? It's her life — why is it my karma?

**Feng:**
**There is no separation. She is a picture of yours.**

**Question:**
She preset her life — her karma — before she came, which led to the illness. That's my karma too?

**Feng:**
She is only a picture of yours. Lester said this many times.

**Question:**
So my mother's illness, my relatives' debts and so on are all my karma.

**Feng:**
**Karma is your desires. Programs are feelings; they are driven by desire.**

**Taking full responsibility means taking complete responsibility for the whole world you create — including the sun rising, the drift of the dust, the vibration of atoms. You created all of it.**

You're just not conscious of it. Stay conscious of it, and you can consciously create what you want, rather than what you don't want.

**Question:**
For example, if some of my relatives and friends are ill, can I help heal them all through releasing? Even someone who's been paralysed for years?

**Feng:**
For questions like “can it do such-and-such?”, I hope you'll all remember something Lester said:

**“*every impossible, no matter how impossible, become immediately a possible, as you completely released on it.*”**
**“Every impossible, no matter how impossible, immediately becomes a possible, once you have completely released on it.”**

I've actually answered this kind of question many times. The answer is always yes.

**Question:**
What exactly does “the harmonious working of the universe”, mentioned above, mean?

For example, if I heal a family member through releasing, is that the harmonious working of the universe?

**Feng:**
At the same moment that you have a thought, she will have the corresponding thought, exactly matching.

So you don't need to use words or actions to make her change her mind.

You only need to deal with your own thinking, and the world will change.

**In the end, there's nothing to do but release. *release only*.**

> **Translator's notes**
> - ***Anāgāmī*** (阿那含, “non-returner”) is the third of the four stages of awakening in Buddhism, and ***arahant*** (阿罗汉) the fourth. The **first fruit** is stream-entry.
> - **The Pure Abodes** (净居天) are the heavens where non-returners are reborn. **Hiranyaloka** is the high astral world described in Yogananda's *Autobiography of a Yogi*.
> - ***Jivanmukta***, ***paramukta*** and ***siddha*** are Hindu terms. Roughly: one liberated while still living; one supremely liberated; and a perfected being.
> - **Nirvana without remainder** (无余涅槃) is final nirvana at the end of the last life, with nothing left to be reborn.
> - The *Dhammapada* verse quoted is verse 218.
