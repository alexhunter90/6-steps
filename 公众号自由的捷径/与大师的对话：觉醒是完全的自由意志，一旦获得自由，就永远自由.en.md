## Conversation 1

**Question:**
God creates a mind, and the mind creates three bodies (the physical body, the astral body and the causal body). Apart from these three bodies, does it also become other things (a stone, a tree and so on)?

**Feng:**
Those are the three bodies particular to human beings. Besides human bodies there are the bodies of other animals, and other matter.

You are God. You created three bodies and created a separate consciousness. You identify with your body, separate from the whole world you project.

**Question:**
Why would we trick ourselves like this? Why choose separation?

**Feng:**
Lester answered this question more than once, and so have I. There's a difference between passive infinity and active infinity.

God before descending is not the same as God returning after going through separation. Once you have actively chosen infinite being, you won't lose it again.

**Question:**
Did God, in descending, actively choose to descend too?

**Feng:**
Mm. Actively experiencing separation.

**Question:**
Oneness choosing to experience multiplicity?

**Feng:**
No. Infinite being isn't oneness in that sense. All creative power comes from infinite being. It's just that infinite being experiences limitation.

**A healthy person experiences illness, and so realises what health means.**

**Question:**
So, for someone who is passively infinite, infinity has no meaning?

**Feng:**
Mm. So-called passive infinity is what you were before you descended. After descending, you can only choose infinity actively.

**Question:**
Once free, free forever? Forever means forever?

**Feng:**
Mm. Once you have gained freedom, you are free forever.

**Question:**
And if you descend again, you're an avatar? Like Jesus, the Buddha, Yogananda?

**Feng:**
Yes.

## Conversation 2

**Question:**
Teacher, last time you said people come to know each other over hundreds of millions of years. Does that mean I (and other unawakened people) have already been reincarnating for hundreds of millions of years?

**Feng:**
Of course — although time doesn't really mean anything.

**Question:**
Lester talked about being freed from millions of years of struggle. Was he deliberately understating it? Hundreds of millions of years — imagine that!

**Feng:**
**Millions of years, billions of years — these are figures of speech.** Natural evolution takes a million years, but **natural evolution won't necessarily bring you awakening.** You can evolve to the point of receiving cosmic consciousness and being in harmony with nature, and then plunge back into delusion again.

**Question:**
Once, Swami Kriyananda asked how long he had been the Master's disciple. The Master said, “A long, long time.”

**Feng:**
Yogananda once traced himself back to when he was a diamond, starting from that dim consciousness.

**Question:**
“Evolving to the point of receiving cosmic consciousness and being in harmony with nature” — even then you can't awaken?

**Feng:**
Mm. That isn't awakening; it's only coming close to the infinite. **Awakening requires an active decision.**

**Question:**
When you trace yourself back to your very beginning, what is it?

**Feng:**
Actually it's without beginning. Time is like a dream; it has no start and no end. Waking up is waking up. Time doesn't have a beginning the way you imagine.

**Question:**
Without beginning or end?

**Feng:**
Until you wake up, it's without beginning or end. After you wake up, it's a dream, and you can enter or leave it at any point in time.

**Question:**
So the suffering and struggle have no end? They're infinite — until you awaken?

**Feng:**
Yes.

## Conversation 3

**Question:**
Teacher Feng, can we wake up in this life?

**Feng:**

> **Translator's note:** Feng answers by quoting Lester in English, followed by a Chinese translation. Lester's English below is exactly as it appears in the original. The Chinese is a faithful translation of it, so it isn't translated again here. A short comment from Feng follows.

**Lester:** We preset the behavior of this physical body before we enter it to put us through experiences that we hope to learn from.

**Q: Knowing that you would attain Realization this time?**

**Lester:** No. Knowing that in past lives you subjected yourself to the law of action and reaction, cause and effect, karma (they're all the same thing), and that you want to continue that game. You did certain things when you were in a physical body before, so next time you want to set up similar things in a hope of undoing some of the things you don’t like, and instigating the things you do like. But you cannot change anything that the body was preset to do by you. You're going to do exactly what you preset for that body before you came into it. There is no free will in worldly living. However, there is a free will. The free will we have is to identify with the real Being that we are, or to identify with the body. If you identify with the body, you're in trouble. So the free will is one of identity. Knowing this, it makes life much easier; you don't fight it. You aim for proper identity.

> **Translator's note:** In the Chinese translation, two phrases are highlighted in red: “in whether you identify with the true Self or with the body, you do have free will” and “free will is a choice of identification”.

**Feng:**
Awakening is entirely free will.

**If someone tells you, “You will definitely awaken,” that must be false too — because it has always been your own decision.**

**Question:**
So if I decide to wake up, nothing can stop me?

**Feng:**
Yes.

---

> **Translator's notes**
> - **Passive and active infinity:** Feng's distinction is between the infinite as it is before it descends into separation (passive, taken for granted) and the infinite consciously chosen again after experiencing limitation (active, never lost again).
> - **Avatar** (阿瓦塔尔): in Hindu thought, a fully liberated being who takes a body again in order to help others.
