The Shortcut to Freedom · posted 8 March 2021, 15:13

**Date of talk: 17 November 2020**

**Feng:**
I failed for six years too.

**Question:**
How? Didn't you find the original Release Method after three years?

**Feng:**
Not releasing constantly is failing. I was stuck.

**Question:**
So could we get stuck for years too?

**Feng:**
Not if you use the Six Steps. Before, I hadn't fully understood the Six Steps. Once I understood them and set out on the path, I didn't get stuck.

**Question:**
Could it be that we haven't understood the Six Steps either? Is that why you feel you have to wait until we really see the Six Steps — otherwise we'll keep on being stuck?

**Feng:**
Mm. It's enough for one person to understand them at the level of experience; then they can answer every question too. It's like what the Buddha said about completing the first turning of the Four Noble Truths and entering the second.

**Question:**
What exactly is “the level of experience”?

**Feng:**
When you first start using the Six Steps, you use them incompletely. You're using the Six Steps to correct your course.

**Question:**
I find I really do want to know what you mean by this. Could you explain it in detail?

**Feng:**
Sure. Actually I explained it in detail yesterday.

> **Translator's note:** The next two passages are from the Āgama sutras, the early Buddhist scriptures, quoted in classical Chinese. They are translated here as literally as English allows.

“Venerable sir! One speaks of ‘true knowledge, true knowledge’. Venerable sir, what is true knowledge? What is it to follow true knowledge?” “Bhikkhu! Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the arising of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, and knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering — this is called true knowledge. Untainted reflection in accord with the Dharma — choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening, observing — this is called right view. “**Lester:** Yes, that's the first step — and it's the first step of the method, isn't it? You must want freedom more than you want the world.

Let me change the wording a little, but this really is how it was at the start. Because it didn't seem to mean much to people, we changed it to ‘You must want freedom more than you want approval and control’, because approval and control equal the world.

But I think it's better to use ‘You must want freedom more than you want the world’. If you do that, what you do next will be: turn around from facing the world, focus back on yourself (*you*), do what needs doing to get out of the world, and allow your infinite being simply to be.

So the first step is: you must want freedom more than you want the world.”

Compare them. If you can read classical Chinese, you'll find they are the same thing. Let me explain simply.

Why do you pursue happiness in this world? Because, at the level of experience, you mistakenly believe there is happiness in the world.

So Lester has you discover that this world equals misery. Fully experiencing this develops an intelligence, which the Buddha called *seeing suffering* — the knowledge that sees suffering.

Next you see the cause of suffering. The world is painful because of your craving and your aversion; as soon as they are extinguished, your suffering vanishes completely. All happiness is within you. This understanding, this intelligence, the Buddha called the knowledge of the arising of suffering and the knowledge of the cessation of suffering.

Knowing that the world is suffering, knowing how suffering arises and knowing how suffering ends — these together make up a correct seeing, which the Buddha called “right view”, also called “true knowledge” and “noble wisdom”.

With right view leading the way, all eight parts of the path appear before you. The Buddha called this the transcendent Noble Eightfold Path. Your direction begins to turn around.

In the Six Steps, this is reaching Step One. So at first the Six Steps are an operating manual; once you truly and fully match Step One, they carry you along.

If you truly achieve Step One, every choice and action you make will naturally tend towards freedom.

“Noble disciple! Suffering, and reflection on suffering; arising…; cessation…; the path, and reflection on the path. Untainted reflection in accord with the Dharma — choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening, observing — this is called right view. It is noble, beyond the world, untainted, free of grasping; it rightly ends suffering and turns towards the end of suffering.”

At this point, every act of your choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening and observing naturally turns around.

It accords with “untainted reflection”. “Untainted” is an old translation term meaning perfect, without flaw.

You no longer look for happiness in the imperfect world, so with your whole body and mind you naturally turn around in every choice.

So reaching Step One of the Six Steps is actually what Buddhism calls a noble one of the first fruit — the fruit of stream-entry, of entering the stream, liberation through wisdom. You have already entered the stream of the noble ones.

You naturally cut off three fetters. The first is the fetter of doubt: because the whole path appears before you, you cut off all doubt.

The second is the fetter of identity view: because you have seen the flaws of the world, you no longer look for an “I” anywhere, and you no longer take the body to be “I”.

The third is the fetter of clinging to rites and rules: because you see the source of everything, you no longer have the idea that rituals or outside powers are effective, or that they can lead you to the end. At the same time, the karma that could have taken you into the lower realms is completely cleared.

This is the result of completing Step One. The remaining five steps then naturally fall into your experience.

Besides in *The Way*, Lester said the same thing in other recordings: “Once you achieve Step One, every action you take will be towards freedom.”

**Question:**
Last night my fetter of doubt came up. I stayed up late reading the autobiography and didn't keep releasing, because I started to doubt the Release Method, doubt myself and doubt what Feng has attained.

Now today I couldn't get out of bed — I'm so tired. Unlike the last two days, I didn't start releasing constantly as soon as I got up. Now I'm a bit stuck too, in apathy, grief and fear.

**Feng:**
Before you really enter the Six Steps, it's completely normal to have doubts. They come and go, again and again.

Until the path appears, you won't truly cut off the fetter of doubt. Once the path appears, the faculty of faith is established — the old translations call it “unshakeable pure faith”. In fact that is achieving Step Two. Since you haven't seen it, of course you'll doubt.

---

**Question:**
How can I turn around and really achieve Step One at the level of experience?

**Feng:**
In the Release Method, the way to turn around is by constantly confirming your *gain*. When you get benefits through releasing, you'll want to release.

For example, say you have an exam today. If in the past you've had the experience of revising hard and passing, you'll revise hard. But if you pass an exam once through releasing, you'll release.

You need to confirm that all your *gain* comes from releasing. *get everything by releasing only*. Getting benefits at the beginning is very important. Let *gain* support you in getting higher *gain*.

Put simply: only when you see that the world = suffering and that all happiness comes from within will you turn around and reach Step One. As long as you still think there is happiness in the world, you'll stop to go looking for it.

If you listen to the audios and watch the videos often, you'll notice Lester frequently asks two questions. One is, “Have you experienced changing the picture inside and seeing the outside change immediately?” The other is, “Have you discovered that all happiness comes from within?”

Only when you really *discover* this will you achieve Step One. In classical texts like the Āgamas it is expressed as seeing suffering and seeing the arising of suffering. But Lester says it casually, in plain language, so you overlook it.

So turning around really doesn't happen by shouting “I want freedom!” It happens by confirming, bit by bit, that your *gain* comes from releasing. Nothing lies outside the Six Steps.

You can't just resist the world or be disgusted with it — that's no use. Unfortunately, when most people shout “I want freedom!”, what they're suppressing is disgust with the world. But disgust and craving are the same thing: they are two directions of desire.

You have to actually achieve Step One at the level of experience for it to work. Through constant releasing, keep confirming your gains. Confirm the source of your happiness.

OK — release. From getting a little benefit, to getting every benefit. There's nothing else to say.

> **Translator's notes**
> - **The three fetters** (三结) — doubt, identity view (taking the body/personality to be the self) and clinging to rites and rules — are the first three of the ten fetters in early Buddhism. They are abandoned at stream-entry.
> - **“The first fruit”** (初果) is the first of the four fruits of the Buddhist path, the fruit of stream-entry.
> - **“The picture inside”** refers to Lester's teaching that changing your inner mental picture changes outer circumstances.
> - **“The autobiography”** is not identified in the text. It may refer to a book about Lester Levenson.
