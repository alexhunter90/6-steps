Use these two: write down your goal, then write down your first thought or first feeling about the goal. Then see which basic want or wants it comes from, write that down, and release those wants, ticking each one off as it's released.

> “The Goal Process exercise will teach you a new way of releasing.
>
> Step 1: At the top of a blank sheet of paper, write your goal — the reworded version.
>
> Step 2: Read your goal, silently or aloud. Under the goal, write the first feeling or first thought that comes to mind — anything related to it.
>
> Step 3: Ask yourself what want lies behind those feelings or thoughts. You can use these questions as a guide: Does it (the feeling or thought) come from wanting approval? From wanting control? Or from wanting to be safe and secure? Note it briefly next to it; you can use abbreviations. If more than one want is stirred up, write them all down.
>
> Step 4: Release all the wants you've written down. Just ask yourself: Could I let go of wanting approval / wanting control / wanting security? When you've dealt with a want, cross it out or tick it.
>
> Step 5: Repeat steps 2–4 until your feeling about the goal becomes courageousness, acceptance and peace. When you feel these higher energies, it means you've removed one layer of limitation related to this goal. Now you have three ways to do step 6.
>
> Step 6: The first way is to keep repeating this process to remove more limitations. The second way is to set the process aside for now and get on with whatever you're doing, releasing as much as you can whenever you happen to think of the goal. [A small warning: before you stop this process, it's best to make sure you've reached one of the three stages of courageousness, acceptance or peace. Otherwise you'll keep being bothered by this incomplete, less-than-ideal result. Also, you may not want to go back to releasing in those lower energies.] The third way is to continue the goal process — that is, to do the action process below.”

**Feng:** Hale's description here basically fits the original teaching; only step 6 is wrong.

> “The Action Steps process is a supplement to the Goal Process. It will help you let go of more inner limitations — whether emotional, psychological or some kind of behavioural block. Anything that stops you taking action or lowers your efficiency in pursuing your goal, let it all go. This process will also help you pick out the actions that really help you pursue a goal, saving you a lot of time and energy.
>
> When it comes to achieving goals and reaching our dreams, many of us have an urge to escape, because we imagine we'll have to do lots of things we don't like. When you release in the Action Steps process, you'll find you can release a lot of resistance to certain actions, or find that some actions aren't actually necessary — they're psychological obstacles you've built yourself.
>
> Right, now please turn to a new page of your releasing journal.
>
> Step 1: At the top of a blank sheet of paper, write your goal. Write it out in full; it's best not to shorten it or use abbreviations.
>
> Step 2: Read your goal silently, then ask yourself: What do I think I need to do to achieve this goal? Then, on a new line, write down every answer you can think of; some will jump straight out. Of course, you can also choose to write down just one thing at a time and do them separately.
>
> Step 3: Next to that thing, write down any thoughts or feelings related to it.
>
> Step 4: Ask yourself what want lies behind those feelings or thoughts. You can use these questions as a guide: Does it (the feeling or thought) come from wanting approval? From wanting control? Or from wanting to be safe and secure? Note it briefly next to it; you can use abbreviations. If more than one want is stirred up, write them all down; you can use abbreviations to save space.
>
> Step 5: Release all the wants you've written down. Just ask yourself: Could I let go of wanting approval / wanting control / wanting security? When you've dealt with a want, cross it out or tick it. Keep repeating and releasing until your feeling about each action step becomes courageousness, acceptance and peace.
>
> Step 6: Repeat steps 2–5 until you've dealt with all the action steps you've written down. If you're short of time, you can do some first and remind yourself to finish the rest later. But it's best to make sure your feeling about each action step reaches the stage of courageousness.
>
> Step 7: Wait for the right moment to act. And don't forget to release before, during and after acting.”

**Feng:** In the releasing of the *To do list*, steps 6 and 7 are wrong; they both add his own material. Achieving a goal requires releasing until the mind naturally leaves the goal and no longer thinks of it, so just keep releasing constantly. You need to release until you feel you completely have it, and then the mind leaves the goal by itself.

Use step 5, but change it slightly. Don't stop when you reach CAP; release until you feel you completely have it, and then the mind naturally stops paying attention to it. Hale used to release to CAP every day and then stop, so he kept going back and forth, and when he wrote his book he put his own mistaken experience into it too.

But the *to do list* can be used as an aid. Its real purpose isn't to make you take action, but to make you release the actions you think you must take. For example, if you think you have to diet to lose weight, release the basic wants behind the action of dieting. That you think you need to open a bank account to get money, or go and view houses to buy a house — all these kinds of limiting ideas.

**Question:** Release this first and then the *to do list*? Or release both together?

**Answer:** Release them together. Release until you don't need to do anything — it's yours right now. As long as there's the slightest doubt, keep releasing. When you've released until you completely believe it, keep releasing until the mind stops paying attention by itself. Or, more simply: release until it falls into your lap. Put that way, you'll understand it better.

> **Goal Chart**
> **Goals I Would Like to Achieve**
> Goal: ______
>
> | What is my NOW feeling about my goal? | Let go? | Feel good? |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | | | |
>
> So word a goal. Write it down. Make it as positive as possible--don't place negative in the goals.
>
> Now have a goal in mind that you want to work on and just follow my words. Look at the goal, see if it brings up a thought or feeling--whatever it is. And could you see that as either a wanting of approval, a wanting of control or a wanting to be safe and secure? And whichever want it brings up, could you let go of it?

![Goal chart (Chinese and English)](2022-05-04-13-51-21.png)

> “Look at your goal again and see what thought or feeling it brings up. Whatever it is, write it down. Could you see it as wanting approval, wanting control or wanting to be safe and secure? Could you let go of these wants? Look at your goal again and see what thought it brings up. Does it stir up your wanting approval, wanting control or wanting security? Could you let go of these wants? Look at your goal again. Does achieving it seem more possible or less possible? If you've really kept releasing, you'll find it's more possible. What other thoughts or feelings came up as you looked at your goal? Do they make you feel you want approval, want control or want security? Could you let go of these ‘wants’? Look at your goal again. What other thoughts came up? Could you see them all as wanting approval, wanting control and wanting security? Could you let go of these wants?
>
> Now look at your goal again. Does it trigger certain thoughts or feelings in you? Whatever it is, could you see it as wanting approval, wanting control and wanting security? Could you let go of these ‘wants’? Look at your goal once more and see what it triggers in your mind. Does it equal the feeling of wanting approval, wanting control and wanting security? Whatever feeling is stirred up, could you let it go? Now keep releasing on this goal until you feel, ‘I have it; it's mine now.’ Then keep repeating the process above, keep releasing on this goal, until it falls into your lap — until you really hold it in your hands (not thinking you already have it, but actually holding it in your hands). If you really keep releasing on a goal, and that goal is truly what your heart wants, it will come very soon.
>
> What if you don't achieve the goal?
>
> If you've released on the same goal for two weeks and it still hasn't worked, I suggest you do the likes-and-dislikes exercise on ‘achieving this goal’. You may find that subconsciously you don't want this result that much, and that's why it isn't happening quickly. But if you're really determined to achieve it, it will come. Lester considered goals extremely important. I suggest you write your goals in a small notebook every day. Sometimes I work on four or five goals at once and release on them every day. Then, when I've really achieved a goal, I circle it and write ‘I did it’. That way, when I look back at the page later, I can see what I achieved and what I didn't. If there's a goal I didn't achieve, I sit down and do the responsibility exercise, ‘Why did I let this happen?’, and then I understand.
>
> I ask myself, ‘Why didn't I achieve this goal?’ Then I try to release, or do the advantages-and-disadvantages exercise on ‘achieving this goal’. Then, through releasing, I can understand what I'm doing. Very often we long for something and strive for it, yet know we can't possibly succeed, so when we're blocked we say, ‘I knew it.’ So make sure you do the likes-and-dislikes exercise and the advantages-and-disadvantages exercise on this kind of goal, according to its depth and intensity. Then release on it, and you'll understand it more clearly. I promise you that as you keep releasing, it will drop from the sky. You must prove it to yourself. Don't believe it just because I say so — prove to yourself that it's true. And don't do it just once; do it again and again.”

**Question:** Is this passage Larry's?

**Feng:** Yes. Larry's account of goals is completely correct.

**Question:** The advantages-and-disadvantages exercise mentioned here — is that releasing too?

**Feng:** It's this one in the workbook, the advantages-and-disadvantages exercise. What is the advantage of this to me? What is the disadvantage? Write it down, see the basic want behind it, and release it. That's the advantages-and-disadvantages process.
