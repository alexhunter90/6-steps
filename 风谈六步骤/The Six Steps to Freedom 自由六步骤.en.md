---

**Step One:** You must want freedom more than you want the world.

You must want freedom more than you want approval and control; wanting approval and wanting control = the world.

**Step Two:** Make the decision to be free.

**Step Three:** All feelings come from wanting approval and wanting control. They are survival programs. Release them.

**Step Four:** Release constantly.

**Step Five:** When you get stuck, release the wanting to change the stuck feeling.

**Step Six:** Each time you release, you are happier, lighter and freer of limitation. As you go on releasing, you become happier and happier, lighter and lighter, and freer and freer of limitation.

**Lester:** *There is no stops in six steps.* (There is no stopping in the Six Steps.)

Note: Because Hale and Larry both later changed the wording of the Six Steps, it's impossible to be sure of the very first, original version. This version is Feng's translation, pieced together from scattered things Lester said about releasing in the advanced course *The Way*. It is offered for reference.

## The six steps

> **English in the original (exactly as it appears), with Claude's translation of the Chinese version given below it:**

1. *English in the original:* Want freedom more than anything else.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* Want freedom more than everything else.

2. *English in the original:* Decide you can do the method and be free.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* Decide that you can gain freedom through releasing.

3. *English in the original:* See all your feelings as expressions of three basic wants: the want of approval, control, and survival. Release the want of approval, the want of control, and lastly the fear of death.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* All your feelings are forms in which three basic wants (wanting approval, wanting control, wanting to survive) express themselves. Release wanting approval and wanting control, until finally you release the fear of death.

4. *English in the original:* Release continuously.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* Release continuously and without a break.

5. *English in the original:* If you are stuck, let go of wanting to change the stuckness.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* If you feel stuck, release the “wanting to change” the stuck situation.

6. *English in the original:* Release more and more and become happier and lighter, until you move beyond happiness into imperturbability and freedom.
   *Claude's translation of the Chinese:* As you release more and more, become happier and lighter, until you go beyond the state of happiness into imperturbability and freedom.

Yiman's note: These Six Steps are a version found online that may be close to the original Six Steps. They're offered only for reference and understanding; for actual practice, Feng's translated version is the main one.

**Question:** If someone is sincere about this but doesn't succeed, will a guru reach out a helping hand?

**Lester:** An enlightened guru can give the greatest help, but if you don't help yourself, he can't help you.

This is the most important point. If you don't help yourself, he can't help you.

When you make the effort to help yourself, he joins you and helps you to the awakening you're ready for. (*When you try to help yourself, He joins you and helps you to a realization that you're ready for.*)

**Question:** He'll help us?

**Lester:** When you're ready to awaken, he'll help you realise it. If you open your heart and receive an infusion of cosmic consciousness, that's because you're ready.

Of all forms of help, the guru's help is the greatest, because he has been there, and he can lead you along the same path.

— *The Way to Ultimate Freedom*, Chapter 32: Enlightenment — Letting Go of the Unconscious

Let me make one key point: don't be afraid of freedom.

Lester often told his students: don't be afraid of freedom. From Lester's time until now, almost everyone who uses the Release Method but doesn't use it to the utmost is afraid of freedom.

The reason you don't release is that you're afraid of losing your feelings, afraid you'll die. In fact you won't.

That's why Lester kept stressing: you won't lose anything; you'll only lose your misery.

The things you do — looking outside for help from “masters” — you should know that what you're doing looks related to freedom, but in fact it's only procrastination.

That includes organising material about me, and sharing things. When you avoid looking within, you'll cook up schemes; you'll do everything except release.

Your mind will tell you it's good for freedom, or good for the world, but it's just making you stop releasing.

Whenever you stop releasing, whatever you do is going in the opposite direction. I keep stressing this: talking about freedom won't make you free; releasing constantly will.

You need to remind yourself: am I on the Six Steps?

— Feng, 11 March 2020 [*sic*; probably 2021]

Once you've released feelings, you won't have compulsively driven thoughts. You'll be free to choose to think what you want, and that will make your world appear as you want it.

The world is only a display — just a screen. When you want to change the picture, smashing the screen is no use. Only releasing helps.

There's nothing out there, only a heap of your thoughts. You created that picture, without exception. The picture is only thoughts; it isn't anything else.

Empty out your feelings, and you have no thoughts; your mind becomes completely quiet. You'll recognise that you are infinite being, and you'll never lose that knowing again. You can put feelings and thoughts in at will, but you won't be run by them any more; you'll use them only to communicate with people.

In fact, as soon as you reach the astral level, you can't see any matter, only bright, blazing light. When you reach samadhi and experience infinite consciousness, there are no feelings or thoughts at all.

Once you're free, you can decide to let the body finish what was preset for it, or leave straight away. That's your choice — an entirely free choice.

— Feng

**Question:** I think it was easier for you to become enlightened because you didn't have many preconceptions beforehand.

**Lester:** Yes. I was lucky, because knowing too much about this subject is an obstacle. The ego uses head knowledge in place of real experience. So I knew nothing about it beforehand, and I was very, very lucky.

**Question:** People all think it's hard, but you didn't know at the time that it was hard.

**Lester:** Yes. But saying this can help you see that you need to let go of the obstacle that head knowledge creates. My experience may encourage you onto this path.

I say: don't believe anything. Start from zero. Build your knowledge on the solid foundation of direct realisation, step by step. Everyone has to come this way.

**Question:** You can't take someone else's experience?

**Lester:** Right. You can go by hearsay, but the only thing that's any use is what you experience yourself.

Take driving a car. If I say I've read books about how to drive, so I already know how to drive — do I really know how to drive? No. Unless I've actually driven a car, I won't know how. The same applies to this path: we have to experience everything for ourselves.
