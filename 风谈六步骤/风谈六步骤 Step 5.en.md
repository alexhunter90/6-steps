**Step Five:** When you get stuck, release the wanting to change the stuck feeling.

> **Lester's original English, as given in the original:** If you get stuck.let go of the wanting to change the stuckness.

## [18 October 2020 — The only reason you get stuck is wanting control]

**Feng:** Step Five is the one you may use most at the beginning, because you'll often get stuck. But being stuck is only wanting control.

The only reason you get stuck is that wanting control is operating. It's that simple. Release the wanting control that is running you, and the stuck feeling immediately turns into flow.

## [23 September 2020 — The more stuck you are, the more you need to release]

The more you run into being stuck, the more you need to release — release the desire to change it. Otherwise you push it down into the subconscious, where it keeps running you, and you stay stuck.

Right now you need to decide to release the wanting control that's keeping you stuck — make a decision that goes beyond it. You have to make a decision to go beyond it before you can become aware of it. Allow it to come up and leave, and then do it.

Don't struggle to suppress it, but don't run away either. Face that stuckness until it's released. Don't go and do something else to escape it.

## [When you're not releasing — that is, when you're stuck — release the desire to control or change the situation]

> **Hale** (translated from the Chinese edition of *The Sedona Method*): **If you really are stuck somewhere, release the desire to control or change being stuck.**
>
> This step is extremely important. It's the safety valve of the Sedona Method, the guarantee that pulls you back when you go astray.
>
> Remember especially: we easily get stuck when we try to change or control what we feel and think. So when we release the desire to change or control what we're feeling right now, our whole state changes too.
>
> Very simply, if any of the following happens, release the desire to change or control the situation:
>
> - There are too many feelings; you can't bear it.
> - You're drifting further and further from releasing.
> - You've forgotten all about releasing.
> - You feel you just can't let go.
> - You can't pin down exactly what you're feeling right now.
> - You feel some particular pattern of thinking or behaviour is especially hard to release.
> - You want to skip the process and release everything quickly.

## [24 October 2020 — Face it, allow it, release it]

**Feng:** Don't be afraid of the habit of getting stuck. Only by allowing it to surface can you release it. When the stuck feeling comes up, you have a strong tendency to want to change it, and your thinking at that moment is driven by that wanting to change. Whenever the head wants to alter the process, that's the reason.

Can habits be solved by suppressing?

Not facing the stuck feeling is pushing it back down.

Habit is nothing but a feeling driving you; programs are feelings. Go back to Step Three of the Six Steps: all feelings are wanting control and wanting approval — release them.

## [18 October 2020 — Because you want to escape a feeling, you cover it with a layer of vague feeling. Release the wanting to change, and the feeling becomes clear]

**Feng:** When you're stuck, you must first release the stuck feeling — release the desire to change it. You can't keep releasing while carrying resistance to being stuck; that's like pressing the accelerator with one foot and the brake with the other.

Not being able to release is being stuck — *stuckness*: a gripping feeling, a feeling that seems glued on and won't leave, or an unconscious urge to run away from it, not daring to touch it. All of these are being stuck.

We tend, when we feel stuck, to do something else and wait until it feels less bogged down before releasing. I've found this isn't right. As Lester said, it pushes it down into the subconscious, where it keeps running you.

*(Feng quoted Lester's words from* The Way *part 1, translated here from the Chinese.)*

> Tell the seminar leader and have him guide you, or keep releasing the stuck feeling. See that when you don't release the stuck feeling, you let it keep operating, and that keeps you stuck.

*stuck* — being stuck, standing still. Any feeling that makes you stop is a stuck feeling; it isn't that there's a special feeling called “stuck”.

Also, when you face the fear of death at the very bottom directly, you'll get stuck. Most people get stuck there.

That includes not being able to see (the feeling) — a *vague feeling*. Because you want to *run away*, you cover it with a layer of vague feeling. Release the desire to change it, release this stuck feeling, and you'll be able to see what's underneath again.

Another example: on some subjects you can't feel any specific feeling. That's being stuck too. Some people say, “I know I want to control something, but I can't feel it.” That's being stuck there.

**I Am Infinite:** For this, you could probably use all sorts of techniques flexibly to stir it up.

**Feng:** That's the *elementary way* — the primary-school way.

What Lester calls the graduate way is releasing the desire to change the stuck feeling. That is: do you feel this feeling is too vague? Do you want to change it? Release the wanting to change it, and the vague feeling instantly becomes clear.

*(Feng quoted Lester's words from* The Way *part 1, translated here from the Chinese.)*

> Just release the stuck feeling, or even the vague feeling. You may not even be able to see it, because you're trying to escape it, or at least you want to change this vague feeling, whatever it is. Once it's gone, you'll be able to walk into the fear of death again.

That's how Lester puts it. Lester specifically talked about unobvious feelings — the *vague feeling*. He specifically talked about it.

When a feeling is vague, you want to change it. Because when a feeling is vague, you want to make it clearer, and that “want” is wanting to change. Once you're aware of it, you can release it. After releasing it, you'll become clear.

I've done this many times. It really is the *postgraduated way* — much simpler and more efficient than the *elementary way* of stirring up feelings with scenarios.

**I Am Infinite:** So the reason I can't feel it is that there's something on top, like the desire to want to change, attached to it and disturbing my perception. Once I release that, I can feel it.

**Feng:** Yes, that's what I mean. The *vague feeling* is covering it. This is the method *The Way* explains again and again.

## [23 October 2020 — When a feeling is vague, release the wanting to change it, and it becomes clear]

**Feng:** With a vague feeling, release the wanting to change it, and it becomes clear.

Every present feeling is wanting control or wanting approval. If you can't make it out, it's a vague feeling. Release the wanting to change it, and it becomes clear.

Once it's clear, you can see whether your present feeling is wanting control or wanting approval, and then you can carry on releasing.

As long as it isn't clear, there's a wanting control. The way to deal with any situation in releasing is in the Six Steps, so don't use methods outside the Six Steps.

Step Three is being aware that all feelings are wanting control or approval, and releasing them. If you can't do this step, you're stuck, and Step Five applies.

Not being able to release constantly is being stuck too; it's driven by wanting control and approval. In the course, the instructor says: “When your feelings well up, don't suppress them, but put more of your attention on your wants.” That is, when releasing, allow the feelings to come up, but be more aware of your “wants”. That's Step Three of the Six Steps.

Be aware that all feelings are driven by wanting control and approval, and release them.

the second, slightly better, is called escaping;

## [23 October 2020 — Control, approval and security aren't bad things, but “wanting” keeps you driven by a sense of lack]

**Feng:** The releasing process is a technique for awakening the instinct to release.

You can control feelings and releasing — in fact Lester used the phrase “control your mind”.

But “effort” (*effort*) is “wanting control”, and wanting control = lack of control.

So as soon as there's effort, wanting control, you'll get stuck.

When you're stuck, a “wanting control” is operating. If you don't release this program that's running you, you'll stay stuck.

When you release this program of wanting control, you feel flow, because programs drive your feelings, and everything that happens next will be under your control.

So it isn't that you can't control feelings and releasing; it's that you need to control them using a specific process and technique, not your old habits.

Control by releasing wanting control; gain approval by releasing wanting approval; gain security by releasing wanting security.

Control, approval and security aren't bad things, but “wanting” keeps you driven by a sense of lack.

Actually, I have a view about personal insights. Test any insight drawn from personal experience against the Six Steps. If it fits the Six Steps, it's right.

Notice Step Three: immediately be aware of whether your present feeling comes from wanting control or wanting approval, and release it.

When your present feeling is wanting control, release the wanting control, rather than letting it stay inside and run you.

If you make an effort to be aware forcefully, to dig, you're doing it with wanting control. You should become aware of this wanting control and release it.

It isn't a question of using the head; it's *how you direct it* — what the direction is. Lester described it as “using the mind to undo the mind”. The key thing is the direction it's pointed in.

**Happy Cat:** Can I understand it as the head giving a direction, and then witnessing the “Infinite” carry it out?

**Feng:** No. It has to fit the releasing process and the Six Steps. It isn't “discarding the head” or “not controlling”; it's “doing what you want through the releasing process”. Lester doesn't say, “You shouldn't want this or want that.” Lester doesn't condemn those “wants”. He says, “Get everything you want through releasing.” So go through the releasing process, fit the Six Steps, keep it simple, and don't mix anything else in.

The process is fixed: let the feeling come up, see whether it's wanting control or approval, and then let it go.

If you don't fit the Six Steps, you can't keep releasing.

If you can't do it, it's because you haven't done it. As long as you do it, you can do it. Keep the Six Steps beside you. Keep it pure, and use the process.

*keep it simple, sweetheart* — that's what Lester called the KISS principle.

Lester said it's very simple, but what people keep doing is making it highly complicated.

We could discuss the same thing in many language systems, translate it into many modes of expression, but none of that is necessary.

What is necessary? Learn the releasing process and the Six Steps. These are all the tools and maps you need on the road to freedom.

**Feng:** The tendency to want control is very strong and the emotions are heavy — that's normal. Releasing in AGF does have some difficulties. Back then I often got stuck too, and talked about being stuck with everyone I met.

**Roller:** *(laughs)* Look at Step Five now.

**I Am Infinite:** I'd guess most people don't know what being stuck is.

**Roller:** Hale calls it resistance.

**[26 July 2021]**

**Sound of Passing Clouds:** Teacher, could you explain this passage in more depth? (The passage about crossing the flood above.) How can I do it in daily life?

My head understands the principle — *release takes no efforts* — but how do I actually do it, with so much going on in life?

**Feng:** This passage is the Buddha's teaching as recorded in the Pali Canon. The principle it sets out is what the Buddha summed up as the Middle Way.

When the Buddha was still Siddhartha, he studied with the various masters of the time and learned the highest stages of meditative absorption. But he realised that the suppressed feelings were like silt sunk to the bottom of a river. When he was in absorption, they were all held down. But when he came out of that intense concentration, feelings and thoughts surrounded him again, like river water stirred up and muddy.

Then Siddhartha went on and heard the teaching of asceticism from the Jain ascetics. He went through six years of austerities, but didn't feel liberated.

The idea of the Middle Way came to him, when he was utterly disappointed, from a man playing a stringed instrument: when the string is too tight, you need to loosen it; when the string is too loose, you need to tighten it. That's how you can understand the passage.

When your releasing stops, remind yourself to release. But when you're suppressing, remind yourself to relax.
