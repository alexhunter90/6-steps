# Appendix 3 — Feng: Follow the Six Steps Through — Either You Finish It in a Month or Two, or You Never Will

**16 December 2020**

**Suile:** While we're releasing, would it be faster to also use the love method from Lester's book *The Power of Love*?

**Feng:** No. In fact Lester told you in the audios that it took him three months, and those three months included time spent exploring. He said that if he had known the Six Steps from the start, it would have taken him only a month or less.

Simple, or impossible.

If you can't do it in one month, and you still can't in two, then freedom is impossible for you.

You can't drag it out for a long time. You can't say, “I'll do a little every day, and in ten-odd years I'll be free.”

Follow the Six Steps all the way through: either you settle it within a month or two, or you'll never do it in this lifetime.

**Zai:** Why can't it be dragged out for a long time?

**Feng:** Because if you're not free after a month or two, then you're not on the Six Steps. And if you're not on the Six Steps, how could you possibly be free?

**Suile:** Just the Six Steps on their own, without combining them with the love practice?

**Feng:** Yes. The Six Steps are the complete map; you don't need to add anything else to them.

Releasing according to the Six Steps, you can reach an extremely high state within a week — even enter *nirvikalpa samadhi*.

If you drag the process out for a long time, then you're definitely not in the Six Steps. Lester tells you that one release is equal to months of meditation, and one release takes only a few seconds. A few seconds are all you need for the equivalent of months of meditation. This method is extremely efficient.

**Everything Connected:** Where are we going wrong? A whole day of releasing doesn't reach the height of one of those releases.

**Feng:** Look at the Six Steps and you'll see whether you're in them. If you're in the Six Steps, your releasing will be constant, and your joy will keep increasing too.

**Everything Connected:** Definitely not Step One. Step Two — is the decision for freedom a decision to be free, or a decision to release?

**Feng:** Make the decision to be free. Decide that you won't stop until you reach freedom. When the Buddha sat under the tree, he decided, “I will not rise from this seat until I find the way to liberation.”

**Zai:** At our level now, can't we set aside Steps One and Two for the time being? We'll only use them once we reach the fruit of stream-entry, won't we?

**Feng:** It isn't about setting aside Steps One and Two. Fully achieving Step One requires stream-entry, but if you don't actively use Steps One and Two, you can't reach stream-entry either. The Six Steps need you to carry them out actively. Then, when you fully achieve Step One, the last five steps carry you along.

**Everything Connected:** But honestly, sometimes I don't want freedom that much.

**Feng:** That's why, for you, the most practical thing is the teaching Lester recommended most: *get everything by releasing only*.

Because you always have wants. Deciding to get every single thing you want through releasing will also keep you releasing non-stop.

> All happiness comes from seeing a little more of infinite being when the mind becomes quiet. You just mistakenly think some person, thing or event outside is giving it to you.
>
> The only source of happiness is your infinite being.
>
> Even the pleasure people get from orgasm is only the mind being quiet for a moment. And what is the happiness of achieving a goal? When you get something, you let the mind be quieter for a while.
>
> When you chase the world, you're developing the mind and making it noisier — that intensifies suffering.
>
> The only way to end suffering is to turn around and let the mind become quiet. Because the only source of happiness is infinite being, and only when the mind is quiet can infinite being show itself more. It's that simple.
> — Feng
