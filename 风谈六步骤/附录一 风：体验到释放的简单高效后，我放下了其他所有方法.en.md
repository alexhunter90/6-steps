> "You will discover that the Sedona Method is the only easy "how to" that the world has today that is effective and proven very quickly by your experience.
>
> Everyone in his or her every act is looking for this ulti-mate state that inheres in us now.  But they are looking for it where it is not -- in the materiality of the world.  This "how to" will enable you to get whatever it is you wish and help you get to the top state. What the world needs now is love more love which is yours now and is proven by your experience in the use of the Sedona Method,  which simply releases the non-love and al-lows your natural existing love to express.
>
> Your life will get healthier, happier, and richer.  Good luck and best wishes for your success. "
>
> Lester

## [14 November 2020]

**Feng:**
I've studied a lot of things — many different teachings. But my experience told me those would take a very, very long time, or wouldn't reach the end. Only the Release Method let me experience efficiency, being able to get there quickly.

I never understood this. I felt other methods ought to be useful too, but my experience told me the Release Method was the only efficient one.

Then I saw this sentence of Lester's and heard a few other things in the recordings, and I understood at once: the Release Method isn't one method among many. It's the only simple, practicable method in the world today — in the whole world, *the only method*. You can judge the “masters” who come out of other methods to be false straight away.

Except those who came back to teach — some yoga masters, for example, many of whom practised very well in previous lives and came back. But it seems even Yogananda's most promising disciple didn't become enlightened through Kriya Yoga.

Kriya Yoga is already the “aeroplane” path — the fastest in yoga. The Release Method is the rocket path, the most direct.

**Go All The Way:** Lester also said that in the future there'll be a method that frees people in an instant.

**Feng:**
There will be in the future, but not in this age. The Release Method didn't appear in the past either, because the world as a whole hadn't reached that stage.

**Roller:** Methods all arise to fit their times.

**Go All The Way:** Mm. Even now, only a few people do the Release Method, and very few of them haven't distorted it.

**Roller:** Pretty much nobody learning the Release Method at the moment takes the Six Steps seriously.

**Feng:**
That's actually very strange. Lester stressed the Six Steps many times, and so did Kam. I've always released on my own, and from the very start I took the Six Steps very seriously.

**Roller:** But most people really only use it to release emotions and improve their lives. As you said, most people's karma decides how seriously they can take this method. Take Step Three of the Six Steps: very few people do it; they're all doing surface-level “squeezing the lemon” releasing, let alone the other steps. Even if you haven't done Step One at first, if you do the last five steps you'll have a huge *move up*.

**Feng:**
Releasing from wants is essential; that's Step Three of the Six Steps. Without it, you can't restore the method's efficiency.

## [1 October 2020]

**Feng:**
I've read a lot about religion, philosophy and meditation myself, but I don't talk about it now, because these conceptual things are often obstacles. When I was studying Buddhism I was very serious about it — I learned Pali to read the Pali Canon.

**Dalu Zheng:** Why did you put Buddhism down?

**Feng:**
Because the Release Method is more efficient for me. Lester said one release is worth months of meditation, and for me that's exactly right, because that's my experience.

My insight meditation used to be pretty good too, but it really isn't as efficient as the Release Method. So I didn't care what it was; I just used whatever was most efficient for me.

Now, when others discuss Buddhism, I generally don't chip in, because I believe this kind of metaphysical discussion is usually useless. Now the discussion focuses on “how to do it” — how to achieve it, how to get there. Even if you study Buddhism, it's like the simile of the raft: you need to know how to cross the river, and focus on that.

I spent many years on study. I used to be quite the knowledge type, but it gave me fewer realisations than the releasing I've done in a short time.

Be a bit more honest with yourself, and you'll see whether you're marking time or moving forward. Taking in conceptual, knowledge-type things easily gives you the illusion that you know a lot, but it's only the head's accumulation.

The head can't understand anything outside the head. It only uses existing experience to guess at something you've never seen.

Imagining and simulating are hugely different from actually seeing. Learning the Release Method is experiential — *experiential*.

I've met many people from the spiritual scene and traditional religions whose heads are crammed with all sorts of views. It's really hard for them to progress.

Today the subject of Xingkong's translations came up. Xingkong was one of the early people spreading the Release Method, and I'm grateful for his work. But there's a problem with what he translated: he worked in too much of the usual spiritual vocabulary. In fact Mr Lester didn't like using those words. Lester liked to put difficult concepts in the simplest language; he liked everyday English. So in translating I also try to keep it everyday and avoid big words.

Lester's style is different from other masters'. He rarely uses words with a heavy classical-religious flavour. But when they translate like that, they put those words back in.

So if I talk about Release Method theory, I set myself a rule: I never use anything outside Lester's system to discuss it. All the language is his own.

I won't use Buddhism to discuss it, even though I've studied Buddhism. I won't mix the teachings of two masters, because I believe each of their paths is complete, and doesn't need us little egos to join and patch them together with our heads.

I used to read Pali side by side with English translations; it took me seven or eight months. I think combining different teachings is quite a strange thing to do. Is it that no master teaches the complete map, and you need to piece it together with your head?

**Mini:** Feng, does the Release Method involve religious faith?

**Feng:** No. Lester said, “Don't believe what I say.” In *The Way* he also quotes Ramana: there is no God, only yourself.

Most importantly, the Release Method doesn't need to impose a belief system on you. You release, and then you gain realisations from within. It isn't like traditional religion, which has you believe a set of things first.

You can have beliefs, but as you release, you'll see the truth for yourself. Then you're not operating at the level of believing but of knowing. You know it's so, without needing a belief system in the mind to believe anything.

Lester also often mentions Jesus Christ, and the line “I am that I am”, which is the best expression of infinite being in the Bible.

**D:** Mini, Lester also said: if you surrender completely, you can even have coffee with Jesus.

**Feng:** Mm. Lester said that as long as you accept it, you can sit and have coffee with Jesus.

I've studied the Bible too, purely as knowledge — from the scholastic system, Augustine, Aquinas, Leibniz's theodicy. Yogananda actually wrote a commentary on a passage of the Bible too. Lester often mentions two enlightened ones: Yogananda and Ramana Maharshi. Of the ancients, he mainly mentions the Buddha and Jesus.

## [15 October 2020]

**Feng:**
I've thought of something funny. When I first learned the Release Method, because it was so simple, my head kept resisting using it.

I thought: “Did I learn Pali, read the canon, learn German to study classical philosophy, and study countless theories of Buddhism, Daoism, philosophy and theology, only to end up using such a simple method?”

I really resisted using the Release Method at the time. The more effective it was, the more torn I felt. Had I studied all those things for nothing?

Now I can answer myself very clearly: yes, for nothing. People who want freedom don't fool themselves.

As I go on releasing, I argue with others less and less, but inside I'm more and more certain.
