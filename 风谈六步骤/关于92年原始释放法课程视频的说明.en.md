## [12 October 2020]

**Feng:** This (the releasing method) is the original process used in the 1992 Sedona Institute videos — the process of the Release Method organisation that Lester founded.

Later Hale and Larry each set up their own organisations, and because they couldn't teach it the same way, they both cut and changed the method. But the original process really is the one that works best.

Originally the Release Method course ran over four days: two days on the first weekend learning to release emotions, and two days the next weekend learning to release wants. So there's no need to linger too long in the stage of releasing emotions. Once you're familiar with it, you can try releasing wants.

The Release Method doesn't need books. There's hardly any theory in the videos; they just keep guiding the students through releasing. Follow along and you'll naturally pick it up, and you'll be amazed at how simple and effective this method is.

Learning from examples of releasing is the simplest way. To learn how the process works, the best thing is to experience it yourself. Watch how others do it and follow along, or have someone guide you, and you'll learn very quickly. That's why you'll see the Sedona Institute teaching very little theory, while the practical part is entirely guided examples. All eight videos keep showing you how other people release.

## [21 September 2020]

At the start, the Release Method really was very simple:

> *identify the feeling.*
> *Feel the feeling.*
> *Could you let go?*
> *Would you?*
> *When?*

The first few (the first four videos) are about using the *workbook*, including setting goals and so on. The fifth (video) is where releasing wants begins. If you don't set goals, you push your wants deep into the subconscious. Setting goals is what lets them come up — this is different from how many people understand it.

A surface “desirelessness” isn't real desirelessness; it's the result of desire being suppressed. *well*, you can find the emotion on the emotions chart. The basic use of the emotions chart is actually very simple.

The earlier ones (the first four videos) are really for newcomers. Once you're familiar with releasing, you still need to trace everything back to wanting control and wanting approval and release those — the course says so too.

As I understand it, the way the earlier lessons are set up is really to get you more *in touch with* your emotions, because most people very easily overlook their emotions. The instinct to suppress is too strong, so several approaches are used to get you to notice your emotions.

For example, at the end of the first lesson Hale gives an example: you have certain particular habits — say you love eating, or smoking, or playing games — and behind all of these there are emotions. Release before these habits push you along.

The second video is about *suppressed feeling*, and about *success*. It gets you to consciously recall when you suppressed emotions, which allows the subconscious to surface. Why do they discuss *success*? Because people have suppressed a great many emotions around the subject of “success”.

The next video should be about goals, and goals make it even harder to avoid emotions. I remember many people saying they have no desires or wants. But ask them, “Isn't there anything you want to achieve? Anything you want? Anything you want to change?” — and when they think about it, it seems there's quite a lot. Look deeper, and it turns out there are emotions behind all of it. So the importance of goals lies in hooking out the emotions you've suppressed.

This is something Lester talks about in the recordings. So the first four videos keep getting you to make contact with emotions tucked away in the corners, and only the fifth video really takes the method deeper, looking at the “want” behind your emotions.
