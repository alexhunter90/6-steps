**Step Four:** Release constantly.

> **Lester's original English, as given in the original:** Make release constant.

## [18 October 2020 — Release constantly; there is no stopping]

**Feng:** Step Four: release constantly. Constant means constant. You do it once, then you can do it a second time, a third, a fourth… until the last time, when you reach freedom.

Lester said, “*there is no stop in the six steps*”. There is no “stop” anywhere in the Six Steps. Constant means constant.

## [24 October 2020 — Release constantly; make it as normal as breathing]

**Feng:** Let me explain Step Four, *make release constant*.

Because until now, suppressing has been your normal state.

So you ask, “Does this need releasing? Does that need releasing?” Do you need to ask, “Do I need to breathe?” Why don't you ask, “Do I need to suppress this?”

Because suppressing is your normal state, releasing has become the abnormal one for you. You need to make releasing your normal state at every moment, like breathing.

Breathing wasn't automatic at first either, and nor were the organs of your body. At first you ran them consciously; only later did you hand them over to programs to run automatically.

You don't necessarily have to stop doing everything, but releasing mustn't stop. As soon as it stops once, it's like a plane stopping on its take-off run: you have to get it running again.

As Lester said: make releasing constant; let it become your new habit (*make release constant, let it become your new habit.*).

First, use all the time you can for releasing. Then you'll find you don't need to “do” anything; things will happen naturally. You watch the body move, so you can keep releasing all the time, without being distracted by operating the body to do things.

Releasing is letting feelings surface and leave. You release once, a new feeling wells up, you release a second time — this is called releasing constantly. If at that point you don't let the feeling leave but push it down, that's called stopping. It's very simple.

Whether or not you're releasing constantly, you can tell for yourself.

## [28 November 2020 — Release once, and again, and again; let feelings rise and float out all day without a break]

**Feng:** Releasing constantly means you release once, then release once more, and then once more. Being stuck is the same: every time you're stuck, you want control, so you release the wanting control. Release each time you're stuck as though it's the first time.

If you really release constantly like this, you'll reach a stage where, when more than 50 per cent of your time is spent releasing, you find it outweighs the habit of suppressing.

When it becomes your new habit, you'll find releasing is as natural as breathing. “As natural as breathing” is just a comparison; it doesn't mean releasing in time with your breath.

> **Lester** (translated from the Chinese; the one sentence given in English in the original is kept): Even when you're with other people, you can keep releasing, continuously, all day without a break. You've probably all experienced the state of (feelings) continually coming up and floating out, coming up and floating out (*You've probably experienced the state where it comes up and floats out, comes up and floats out.*). You must reach this and do this.
>
> Really, there's so much “garbage”. Occasional releasing has some benefit too. But if you want to release all of it — lots of releasing, constant releasing — look at the Six Steps. You must keep releasing constant. All day long, keep letting (the feelings) float up and drain out. You become aware of some feeling and release it. It's a bit like sitting and watching it, watching it keep going.

It comes up and floats out (*comes up and floats up*). It comes up and floats out, without stopping.
