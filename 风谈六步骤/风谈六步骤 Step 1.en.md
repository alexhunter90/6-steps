**Step One:** You must want freedom more than you want the world.

> **Lester's original English, as given in the original:** You must want freedom more than you want the world(approval and control).

(Chinese gloss in the original:) You must want freedom more than you want approval and control; wanting approval and wanting control = the world.

> **Lester:** Yes, that's the first step — and it's the first step of the method, isn't it? You must want freedom more than you want the world.
> Let me change the wording a little, but this really is how it was at the start. Because it didn't seem to mean much to people, we changed it to “You must want freedom more than you want approval and control”, because approval and control equal the world. But I think it's better to use “You must want freedom more than you want the world”.
> If you do that, what you do next will be: turn around from facing the world, focus back on yourself, do what needs doing to get out of the world, and allow your infinite being simply to be. So the first step is: you must want freedom more than you want the world.
> — From *The Way*, part 1

## Question: How can you turn around and really achieve Step One at the level of experience?

**[17 November 2020] Feng:** In the Release Method, the way to turn around is by constantly confirming your *gain*. When you get benefits through releasing, you'll want to release.

For example, say you have an exam today. If in the past you've had the experience of revising hard and passing, you'll revise hard. But if you pass an exam once through releasing, you'll release.

You need to confirm that all your *gain* comes from releasing.

*get everything by releasing only*. Getting benefits at the beginning is very important. Let *gain* support you in getting higher *gain*.

Put simply: only when you see that the world = suffering and that all happiness comes from within will you turn around and reach Step One. As long as you still think there is happiness in the world, you'll stop to go looking for it.

If you listen to the audios and watch the videos often, you'll notice Lester frequently asks two questions. One is, “Have you experienced changing the *picture* inside and seeing the outside change immediately?” The other is, “Have you discovered that all happiness comes from within?”

Only when you really *discover* this will you achieve Step One. In classical texts like the Āgamas it is expressed as seeing suffering and seeing the arising of suffering. But Lester says it casually, in plain language, so you overlook it.

So turning around really doesn't happen by shouting “I want freedom!” It happens by confirming, bit by bit, that your *gain* comes from releasing. Nothing lies outside the Six Steps.

You can't just resist the world or be disgusted with it — that's no use. Unfortunately, when most people shout “I want freedom!”, what they're suppressing is disgust with the world. But disgust and craving are the same thing: they are two directions of desire.

You have to actually achieve Step One at the level of experience for it to work. Through constant releasing, keep confirming your gains. Confirm the source of your happiness.

OK — release. From getting a little benefit, to getting every benefit. There's nothing else to say.

## [3 December 2020 — You turn around only when you see that happiness comes entirely from within]

**Feng:** The main thing is to achieve Step One. Once you achieve Step One, everything after it is achieved. The main thing is that the direction of flow changes completely.

You haven't noticed the other wording of Step One: wanting freedom more than wanting control and approval — *more than*. You can want control and approval, but you want freedom more than them.

I posted what Lester said explaining “entering the stream”:

> **Lester:** *(the passage from* The Way *part 1 quoted at the top of this chapter)*

**Feng:** Achieve Step One, and you'll fall into the remaining five steps — Lester really did say that. Compare the ancient and modern expressions (Lester's words quoted above and the two Āgama passages below).

> “Venerable sir! One speaks of ‘true knowledge, true knowledge’. Venerable sir, what is true knowledge? What is it to follow true knowledge?” “Bhikkhu! Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the arising of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, and knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering — this is called true knowledge. To be thus is to follow true knowledge.”

“Arising” means the cause; the arising of suffering is the cause of suffering. Knowing suffering, knowing the cause of suffering, knowing that suffering can be ended, knowing the way to end suffering — this is the first turning of the Four Noble Truths. This knowing is called “true knowledge”. True knowledge is like switching on a light in a room. At that point the “transcendent Noble Eightfold Path” appears before you immediately — the knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering.

> “Noble disciple! Suffering, and reflection on suffering; arising…; cessation…; the path, and reflection on the path. Untainted reflection in accord with the Dharma — choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening, observing — this is called right view. It is noble, beyond the world, untainted, free of grasping; it rightly ends suffering and turns towards the end of suffering.”

Your every choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening and observing naturally turns around (this is called “turning towards the end of suffering”) and accords with the untainted. “Untainted” is an old translation term meaning perfect, without flaw. You no longer pursue tainted (imperfect, flawed) happiness. In the seven factors of awakening, this stage runs from the awakening factor of mindfulness to the awakening factor of investigation. In the Six Steps, it is Step One and Step Two.

But no school of Buddhism today still knows this. Look up what any school of Buddhism says about the seven factors of awakening, and it's wrong. Yet Lester explains this in plain language, very clearly.

Put simply: only when you see that the world = suffering and that all happiness comes from within will you turn around and reach Step One. As long as you still think there is happiness in the world, you'll stop to go looking for it.

If you listen to the audios and watch the videos often, you'll notice Lester frequently asks two questions. One is, “Have you experienced changing the *picture* inside and seeing the outside change immediately?” The other is, “Have you discovered that all happiness comes from within?” Only when you really *discover* this will you achieve Step One. In classical texts like the Āgamas it is expressed as seeing suffering and seeing the arising of suffering. But Lester says it casually, in plain language, so you overlook it.

As long as you keep open the possibility of finding happiness in the world, you won't achieve Step One, because wherever you think happiness can be found, you'll decide to go there. All happiness comes from within, but you really need to *discover* this for yourself — you need to experience it. You can't just resist the world or be disgusted with it; that's no use.

## [17 November 2020 — Step One of the Six Steps corresponds to stream-entry in the Buddhadharma]

**Feng:** Why do you pursue happiness in this world? Because, at the level of experience, you mistakenly believe there is happiness in the world.

So Lester has you discover that this world equals *miserable*. Fully experiencing this develops an intelligence, which the Buddha called *seeing suffering* — the knowledge that sees suffering. Next you see the cause of suffering. The world is painful because of your craving and aversion (craving and aversion are the two directions of your desire — the three wants: wanting security, wanting approval, wanting control). As soon as they are extinguished, your suffering vanishes completely. All happiness is within you.

This understanding, this intelligence, the Buddha called the knowledge of the arising of suffering and the knowledge of the cessation of suffering. Knowing that the world is suffering, knowing how suffering arises and knowing how suffering ends — these together make up a correct seeing, which the Buddha called “right view”, also called “true knowledge” and “noble wisdom”. (Knowing suffering, knowing the cause of suffering, knowing that suffering can be ended, knowing the way to end suffering — this is the first turning of the Four Noble Truths, that is, reaching stream-entry.)

With right view leading the way, all eight parts of the path appear before you. The Buddha called this the “transcendent Noble Eightfold Path”, and your direction begins to turn around. In the Six Steps, this is reaching Step One. So at first the Six Steps are an operating manual.

At this point, your every choosing, discerning, investigating, knowing, keen wisdom, awakening and observing naturally turns around. Your whole body and mind turn towards liberation, in accord with untainted reflection. “Untainted” is an old translation term meaning perfect, without flaw. You no longer look for happiness in the imperfect world, so with your whole body and mind you naturally turn around in every choice.

So reaching Step One of the Six Steps is actually what Buddhism calls a noble one of the first fruit — the fruit of stream-entry, of entering the stream, liberation through wisdom. You have entered the stream of the noble ones, and you naturally cut off three fetters:

The first is the fetter of doubt: because the whole path appears before you, you cut off all doubt.

The second is the fetter of identity view: because you have seen the flaws of the world, you no longer look for an “I” anywhere, and you no longer take the body to be “I”.

The third is the fetter of clinging to rites and rules: because you see the source of everything, you no longer have the idea that rituals or outside powers are effective, or that they can lead you to the end.

At the same time, the karma that could have taken you into the lower realms is completely cleared. This is the result of completing Step One; the remaining five steps then naturally fall into your experience.

Besides in *The Way*, Lester said the same thing in other recordings: “Once you achieve Step One, every action you take will be towards freedom.”

Before you really enter the Six Steps, it's completely normal to have doubts; they come and go, again and again. Until the path appears, you won't truly cut off the fetter of doubt. Once the path appears, the faculty of faith is established — the old translations call it “unshakeable pure faith”. In fact that is achieving Step Two.

## [11 December 2020 — Only by making contact with infinite consciousness again and again through releasing, and feeling that it is the only source of happiness, will you turn around completely]

**Feng:** When Lester reached samadhi, he didn't know there was any such thing as samadhi. So whether or not you know the concept of samadhi doesn't affect your getting there.

What I keep explaining is that *nirvikalpa samadhi* is actually the full achievement of Step One of the Six Steps.

Why? Because you only fully achieve it at the moment you make contact with the infinite.

Making contact with the infinite again and again through releasing, you suddenly find that those seeds have no effect any more; all your motives for looking for happiness elsewhere are gone. Because you have discovered, at the level of experience, the only source of happiness, the seeds no longer take effect.

If you don't make contact with infinite consciousness, you'll always feel there is happiness in the world.

And as long as you feel there's happiness in the world, your direction may turn towards the world again. It's that simple.

> **Lester:** I won't give you any more pointers right now; I've given you too many already. How do you feel about what I've said? So far, how do you feel about what I've given you? These are the key points:
>
> The first is: you want freedom more than you want the world.
>
> The second is: get all your happiness from within, through releasing. The next is: decide on freedom, and do it.
>
> And the next: release the fear of dying directly. For you, this is the hardest one, because you feel that if you release it, you'll die.
>
> — From *The Way*, part 4

> The first lesson is that you must want freedom more than you want the world. Following from that, you should get all your happiness from within yourself — get it just through releasing.
>
> — From *The Way*, part 2

**Feng:** Getting all your happiness from within — fully doing it — is the attainment of *nirvikalpa samadhi*.

Lester says it so ordinarily that people overlook what he means.

Before you've completely released the mind, you get a preview of infinite being. That is, even though you haven't yet released the whole program of the mind, there are moments when the mind steps back. At such a moment your mind is completely still, temporarily, so you can experience infinite being in that moment and get a preview of it. This can greatly increase your longing for freedom.

Because you'll find that whatever you get in the world can't match a ten-thousandth of that happiness. Every moment in the world, compared with that state, is torment.

After that, your releasing is different. You no longer need to remind yourself of the Six Steps, because you're in Step One; every decision you make naturally points towards freedom. You can't stop releasing at all, and you'll often enter samadhi, often make contact with the infinite, and each time it takes away many of your desires.

I've said before that, reaching *nirvikalpa samadhi* through certain methods, your habitual tendencies are a little worse off, so it takes more time to release karma. That's why Lester took three months; the process included working out the method of releasing.

If you don't use the Release Method and only burn up karma by entering the infinite, it will take even longer.

The spiritual scene likes to talk about “practising after awakening” — “holding the realisation, and matching understanding with practice”. These are actually Zen concepts. Because Zen is an intellectual method, you need to hold on to it: in every part of life, you use infinite consciousness to correct your habitual tendencies, recognising that you are not the doer but that your infinite being is acting through you. This process is the process of releasing karma.

Releasing karma only happens when you recognise that you are not the doer; it can't happen through action itself.

**Roller:** So “after awakening” really means after becoming a noble disciple.

**Feng:** Of course. Buddhism makes this distinction quite precisely. Before the fruit of stream-entry, whatever supernatural powers or states you have, you can only be called an ordinary person, in the stream of ordinary people.

After stream-entry, you're in the stream of the noble ones — a noble disciple.

Gods of the desire realm, the form realm and the formless realm are all ordinary beings.

## [14 November 2020 — The longing for freedom is not built on disgust with the world]

**Feng:** Lester said that aversion is usually suppressed more deeply.

Aversion to the world, like craving for the world, is wanting the world. So, when it comes to releasing on goals, you have to actually achieve them.

If you can't achieve your goal, there must still be an “I can't” program suppressed deep down.

So when Lester stresses “getting everything through releasing”, you need to really do it, not replace it with a limiting idea.

*(Roller then quoted a passage from* Get High to Release*.)*

> **Lester:** It's very hard to see aversions to the world (*aversions*). Your cravings (*attachments*) are obvious, and you're chasing them all the time, but the aversions keep you shut out.
>
> So when you go after a goal, “oh, I'm afraid”, “I can't” and all those things come up.
>
> So the aversions to the world come to the surface, and then you can let them go. If you aren't aware of it, you won't be able to see it or deal with it. So achieving things in the world brings out your negative programs and thoughts.
>
> The only reason the world can't provide you with wealth and abundant service is that you have opposite thoughts, and they're suppressed.
>
> Work to make the world better, let the opposite feelings come to the surface, and then release them. Because if you can't succeed, it's because you persist in using programs that let you down, without seeing them.

**Feng:** For every craving there is an aversion; *attachment and aversion* come in pairs.

Aversion is the side of desire. This is mentioned in *The Right Path*: *desire (attachment) and aversion*.

The workbook has *want* and *don't want*. What you want is craving; what you don't want is aversion.

But both are a kind of “wanting” — just two different directions, *different direction*.

## [17 November 2020 — Use goals to hook out suppressed feelings, and confirm through releasing that all your gains come from the infinite within; only then will you tend towards freedom and achieve Step One]

**Feng:** If there's something you want, go and achieve it. That's what Lester teaches you. The more you get, the more you'll uncover the real source of happiness. When you really achieve Step One, you don't have to think about whether you want freedom or the world at all; every action and choice you make from then on will naturally tend towards freedom.

But as long as you still keep open the possibility that “there's happiness in the world”, you won't achieve it. You'll unconsciously go looking for happiness somewhere else, and that becomes your goal — and you need to use that goal to pull the programs out. Only by achieving goals will you discover that the programs have been running you all along, and only then will your direction truly turn around.

If you suppress it, it stays inside, affecting you, and you'll never achieve Step One.

Step One isn't a slogan; the Six Steps are a perfect map. You need to confirm what you gain through releasing, until you discover that all happiness comes from one thing — infinite being. You discover, as Lester said, *the world is completely miserable*.

When you achieve a goal, you'll find you didn't get the happiness you imagined. Then you'll stir up the next goal, the next want for the world. When you achieve that, you find you still haven't found the happiness you wanted, so your desire moves on to the next goal after that.

You keep rising, keep confirming the source of happiness, until you experience that the source of all happiness is your “mind quietening down, letting more of the infinite being within show through”. Then your direction turns around. That is Step One completely fulfilled, and freedom becomes your only goal.

After that, as Lester said, you automatically fall into the remaining five steps — that's all.

## [19 November 2021 — Once you truly reach Step One, you don't fall back]

**Feng:** No longer tempted by ego-consciousness, tending completely towards freedom — this is Step One of the Six Steps. In yoga they call it *nirvikalpa samadhi*: the start of the real work.

If you haven't reached this point, you may fall back. If you have reached it, you won't fall back. Truly reaching Step One, you won't sink back down. Entering the stream (stream-entry): no more going backwards.

Insight into the source of happiness will bring you to Step One. After that, every decision you make is inward — each one is undoing your bonds and moving towards your real being. The most crucial thing is that you're used to getting things by struggling in the world. You have to discover that this is foolish — release, and then get things effortlessly — and make that your normal state.

The sheets are very good tools, and I recommend you use them too (meaning the releasing sheets in the workbook). What I strongly recommend is the goal sheet — and I suggest adding a record of failures.

You have to discover your ability to achieve every goal. You have to remove your failure programs, and the precondition for removing them is facing them and allowing them to surface.

> If you truly achieve Step One, every choice and action you make will naturally tend towards freedom, and then you'll decide on freedom.
> Otherwise, why would Lester and Kam say that once you reach Step One, nothing can stop you? Because Step One is *nirvikalpa samadhi*: you won't fall back, and the seeds no longer have any effect.
> — Feng, 3 December 2020

## [28 November 2020 — How to reach Step One quickly]

**Feng:** I knew deeply what the Āgamas mean by “stream-entry” — stream-entry, entering the stream. When you fully achieve Step One of the Six Steps, every inclination and choice leads towards freedom.

The knowledge that sees suffering is the forerunner of achieving Step One. You see that the direction of the world = suffering, and that the direction of freedom — infinite being — is the only source of happiness. Then you spontaneously stop all your attempts to find happiness in the world by bashing your head against it until it bleeds.

**Question: How can you quickly reach Step One in actual experience — that is, the stage of stream-entry?**

**Feng:** Follow the Six Steps all the way through. Of the Six Steps, the one people most easily overlook is Step Six, but Step Six is exactly the key to achieving Step One.

Because, in your actual experience, you are looking for happiness in the direction of the world, you need to put into practice Lester's favourite teaching: *get everything by releasing only*. Every time you want something, get it through releasing; that gain will make you want to release even more.

Keep confirming your *gain*, and it will keep you releasing constantly — until you find that your goals are being achieved one after another, and you still aren't happy. Then you will “discover”, in your actual experience, that nothing at all can bring you happiness.

Instantly, you will become aware of the one and only source of happiness. At that moment, the direction of flow changes immediately — a complete change in the direction your life flows. You have turned around completely. That is achieving Step One fully: wanting freedom more than wanting the world.

After that, just as Lester said, you keep falling into the remaining five steps.

> Fully achieving Step One requires stream-entry. But if you don't actively use Steps One and Two, you can't reach stream-entry either. The Six Steps need you to carry them out actively. Then, when you fully achieve Step One, the last five steps carry you along.
> That's why, for you, the most practical thing is the teaching Lester recommended most: *get everything by releasing only* (getting everything just by releasing).
> *everything means everything* (everything is everything). Getting everything you want just by releasing — this too lets you follow the Six Steps through.
> — Feng, 16 December 2020

**Feng:**

Verifying is achieving Step One. Only when you achieve Step One can you cut off doubt, and only once doubt is cut off can it be called “verifying”. The whole path appears before you. You know directly how to go on from here to the end — a direct knowing that doesn't come through thinking. Then you find that none of your actions can go in the direction opposite to freedom any more. This feeling of the flow changing direction is extremely clear.

**I Am Infinite:**
How did you suddenly verify the Six Steps?

**Feng:**
You mean know the Six Steps directly?

**I Am Infinite:**
Mm. How long did you release continuously?

**Feng:**
I released for less than a week — releasing constantly.

**I Am Infinite:**
In retreat, releasing for a whole week?

**Feng:**
Mm. My constant releasing wasn't sitting there blankly, forcing myself to release. I had written lots of goals down. My mental activity was testing.

This is what it was like for me: I'd do a release and feel very good. Then I'd say to myself, “If I release once more, will I feel even better?” Testing Step Six. Then I'd get stuck, and I'd say, “Let's test Step Five. Release the wanting control and see whether it really gets better.” I'd look — ha, sure enough, it was flowing again. So happy. Then release once more.

So the whole process was actually quite fun. It wasn't the grim, ascetic sitting that many people imagine. After releasing constantly for a few days, I already felt very happy, and at that point insights arose naturally. I just knew directly what the only source of happiness is.

Suffering is limiting your infinite being. When you cover it up with feelings, that is suffering.

The moment this realisation happened, I immediately felt that something had changed. Releasing became very active — it no longer needed any enticement from those goals on paper. I just kept releasing and releasing, and then I reached the point I've talked about: I suddenly pulled free of the whole mind. It felt as though all the force my mind was using turned inward. In that single instant it felt as though my whole body and mind had turned around, and then I suddenly knew all the steps that came after.

At the time I thought those experiences were personal to me, so I didn't describe many of them in detail.

For example, when the pressure in my body lifted, I suddenly relaxed completely and sat bolt upright.

Now I know this is bound to happen, because it is a precondition for entering samadhi, and also one of the earlier limbs leading to *nirvikalpa samadhi*.
