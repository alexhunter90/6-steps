**▲ Topic: Constant releasing is important — and simple**

**Note: This piece is put together from things Feng shared at different times.**

**Feng:** Constant releasing is simple: you just release the three wants. Whatever feeling comes up doesn't matter. What matters is whether you want control, want approval or want security.

In fact, as long as you spend more than 50 per cent of your waking time each day releasing, you'll find that the momentum of releasing gradually becomes greater than that of suppressing. Then you'll keep on releasing. You'll release quite naturally; releasing becomes as constant as breathing, and it will reach twenty-four hours a day.

**Question:** Feng, in these two weeks, have you released more than in the past two years?

**Feng:** It isn't a question of quantity; it's a question of momentum. Releasing is like a plane on its take-off run. If you keep starting and stopping, you'll never get off the ground. Only if you charge straight ahead can you build up enough speed to take off.

Constant releasing is simple. Release once, release again, release again… and then, like a plane lifting off after its run, feelings will keep welling up in a steady stream to be released.

Constant means not stopping. You may not understand that now, because you're working hard at releasing. But once releasing becomes your normal state, it's as natural as breathing. As long as you decide to reach freedom, feelings will keep rising up and flowing out.

When you release constantly, feelings surge out in waves, and you just watch them drain away. When the want behind a feeling is identified, it is released immediately — you hardly need to ask the next question. Sometimes it happens so fast your mind doesn't have time to understand.

When a want is brought out, it's released. If it isn't, go back to the start of the process: relax your body, feel it again, identify which want it is, and let it go.

Keep it simple and easy; repeated practice will make you skilled. Thinking and analysing will make it harder and harder to release, because the mind can only replay past experience.

At first your habit of suppressing is stronger than your habit of releasing, and you need to go through the process again and again. Once you make releasing your normal state, constant releasing happens.

If you're tired, you're forcing it. Releasing doesn't need force. When you really start releasing wanting control and approval, you'll find it's very simple.

Every time you try to force, that's wanting control. The instant you notice it, you can let go. The moment you let go, you immediately feel lighter and the resistance lessens. You don't need any other guidance or procedure; you only need to become aware of the wanting control and allow it to be released.

Look at your feeling right now. Is what's being stirred up wanting control or wanting approval? Can you become aware of it? Can you allow it to be released? Is there more? Can you allow it to be released? You'll see these wants are like waves, continually pushing themselves out. Every time a want is released, countless emotions stuck to it dissolve.

Allow the feeling to be noticed, and allow it to leave. The feelings themselves want to get out too: they are *suppressed energy*. So all you need to do is allow them to leave.

> **Translator's note:** The next passage of Lester's is translated from the Chinese; his original English isn't in the repository.

**Lester:** Did you notice the word “allow”? That's how it happens. You stop keeping them down; you allow them to come up and leave. Because, every moment, it too is trying to push itself up and out — every suppressed feeling is.

So when you do it, it's very light and easy. They come up and leave like waves, until the waves stop, and then you're finished.

Constant means constant. You do it once, then you can do it a second time, a third, a fourth… until the last time, when you reach freedom. Lester said, *“there is no stop in the six steps”*. There is no “stop” anywhere in the Six Steps. Constant means constant.

In the Six Steps, you can release constantly. Out of the Six Steps, you'll stop. Keep them close by and remind yourself at all times. If you're in the Six Steps, your releasing will be constant, and your joy will keep increasing too.

If every release brings you lightness and joy, then you're releasing — constantly releasing your feelings, constantly getting lighter and happier. A clear sense of energy leaving, along with a quieter, happier head — that is releasing.

If your releasing stops, that's being stuck.

You want to release, but the releasing stops — that is called being stuck. It shows that the program of “wanting control” has surfaced, which is exactly the moment to release it. The more stuck you are, the more you need to release, because that's how you dissolve the stuck program. First allow yourself to become aware of the stuck feeling. Then become aware that you want to change it. Lightly allow yourself to become aware that you want to change it, and it dissolves. Then look at your feeling now. If it's still stuck, keep allowing yourself to become aware of it, then of the wanting to change it. And the wanting to change will keep leaving.

Because you've suppressed so much, you need to release constantly. But if you keep releasing the wanting to change and release it cleanly, this stuck program will disappear completely and won't come back. From then on, releasing will mostly flow very smoothly and you won't get stuck any more. Doesn't that sound great?

Being stuck may still come up once or twice, because some stuck programs are suppressed very deeply, but it won't be a normal state any more.

When you feel frustrated with releasing, it's a hard stage — I know, because you'll resist releasing. First, be clear that you want to be freer. Then keep deciding to release. (Notice your resistance and reluctance, and release the want behind it.) Release only what's most on the surface. Once that's released, decide again to release what's most on the surface. Keep it constant by deciding to release, one time after another.

Aversion is a feeling. When you feel aversion, that's immediately wanting control. When you release the wanting control, the feeling of aversion dissolves too. Try it.

Whenever you stop releasing, whatever you do is going in the opposite direction (from freedom). I keep stressing this: talking about freedom won't make you free; constant releasing will.

*the world is only misery* — that's how Lester put it. The world is only suffering; that's how the Buddha put it too. It's simply the fact. The world is only the mind's imagination, and the mind is nothing but limitation. When you limit your infinite being, you suffer.

**Question:** Then where does joy come from?

**Feng:** All happiness comes from seeing a little more of infinite being when the mind becomes quiet. You just mistakenly think some person, thing or event outside is giving it to you.

The only source of happiness is your infinite being. Even the pleasure people get from orgasm is only the mind being quiet for a moment. And what is the happiness of achieving a goal? When you get something, you let the mind be quieter for a while.

When you chase the world, you're developing the mind and making it noisier — that intensifies suffering. The only way to end suffering is to turn around and let the mind become quiet. Because the only source of happiness is infinite being, and only when the mind is quiet can infinite being show itself more. It's that simple.
