**The Six Steps to Freedom**

## Shared on 30 September 2020

**Feng:** Lester liked to put difficult concepts in the simplest possible language, so in translating I also try to keep it everyday and avoid big words. I can't even find the original version of the Six Steps. (Note: the Six Steps found in Larry's and Hale's books and in the workbook have all been modified to some degree from the original.) Lester never gave a single, focused talk on the Six Steps. He mentioned them here and there, or just referred to the *six steps*, but if you piece it together you get more or less the whole thing. Let me try translating them afresh, using his own words:

- **Step One:** You must want freedom more than you want the world. (You must want freedom more than you want approval and control; approval and control = the world.)
- **Step Two:** Make the decision to be free.
- **Step Three:** All feelings come from wanting control and wanting approval. They are survival programs. Release them.
- **Step Four:** Release constantly.
- **Step Five:** When you get stuck, release the wanting to change the stuck feeling.
- **Step Six:** Each time you release, you are happier, lighter and freer of limitation. As you go on releasing, you become happier and happier, lighter and lighter, and freer and freer of limitation.

**Feng:** There — these are the Six Steps, pieced together from Mr Lester's scattered words.

**Feng:** These are the Six Steps from the 1992 workbook. They're much the same either way; use whichever wording you prefer.

- **Step One:** You must want imperturbability more than you want approval and control.
- **Step Two:** Decide that you can do this method and reach imperturbability.
- **Step Three:** See that all emotions come from two wants: wanting approval and wanting control. See it immediately, and immediately release the wanting approval and control.
- **Step Four:** Make it constant. Release all your wanting approval and control, whether you are alone or with other people.
- **Step Five:** If you are stuck, let go of your wanting to control the stuckness.
- **Step Six:** Each time you use this method, you will feel lighter and happier. If you keep releasing, you will keep feeling lighter and happier.

> **Translator's note:** For each step, the [Six Steps to Freedom](../Six%20Steps%20to%20Freedom%20自由六步.en.md) page puts Lester's own English for both versions next to Feng's Chinese.

## Shared on 16 October 2020

**Feng:** Keep the Six Steps close by. Every step needs to be taken seriously, and every step can be used to remind yourself. These are the fragments where Lester mentions the Six Steps, which I pieced together from several of his recordings:

> 1. You must want freedom more than you want the world(approval and control). 2. Make the decision to go free. 3. All feelings culminate in two wants: wanting of approval and wanting of control. 4. Make release constant. 5. If you get stuck, let go of the wanting to change the stuckness. 6. Each time you release, you are happier and lighter. You have more motivation to go free.

I've translated two versions: one is the 1992 course version, and the other is pieced together from Lester explaining the Six Steps in his recordings. Both versions work and they're much the same; use whichever you like. The course version is a bit more formal; Lester's version is a bit more concise.

**Roller Coaster:** I feel that sometimes reading the English makes it clearer.

**Feng:** If you can read English, of course it's best to read the English. There are some sentences I find hard to translate, because that concise, direct force of Mr Lester's is very hard to carry over.

**“I Am Infinite”** (group member): The two versions seem much the same.

**Feng:** Not very different. They just changed “freedom” to “imperturbability” and changed “change” in Step Five to “control”; everything else is basically the same. In the advanced courses Lester liked to say *freedom*, but in the course they probably felt it didn't mean much to newcomers, so they changed it to *imperturbability*.

As Lester explained it, *imperturbability* is a state in which no person or thing can possibly disturb you — absolute serenity.

(Note: Hale's book talks about releasing the desires “wanting separation” and “wanting oneness”. This is incorrect and doesn't match the original Release Method. To avoid confusing those who come to releasing later, Feng's clarification of this point is included below.)

**Feng:** He (Hale) added in the *saparation* (separation) that Lester talked about elsewhere — it should be in the *Goals and Winning* recording. Lester did talk about *saparation* and saw it as the most fundamental thing, but it isn't something to be deliberately released in the method. Lester has you release only three things. First release the two big wants, wanting approval and wanting control; then the fear of death (wanting to survive / wanting security) will come up by itself for you to release. After that, the whole drive of the mind is gone. The mind is completely quiet, and you no longer identify with your *body mind* but with your *infinite beingness*. Then this *saparation* (separation) ends, and there is only *oneness*. Hale's adding separation and oneness to the wants that releasing targets doesn't fit the method. Just release the two wants. If you often feel wanting security, then release the three wants. The eighth video of the course asks about all three wants together; perhaps within just a few days they'd already come into contact with wanting security. The key is to release only wanting control and wanting approval and not bother about anything else, because all feelings are driven by them. Once you've released the two wants, those feelings dissolve by themselves.
