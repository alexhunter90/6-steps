## ► How to use Step One

Right now, do I want freedom, or do I want approval and control?

You can want control and approval, but you want freedom more than them. If, right now, you want control and approval more than you want freedom, you won't release.

Use Step One actively to remind yourself to release.

## ► How to fully achieve “wanting freedom more than the world” — how to turn yourself around towards freedom

### Sharing 1: By releasing constantly, keep confirming your gains and the source of your happiness (shared 17 November 2020)

**Feng:** In the Release Method, the way to turn around is by constantly confirming your *gain*. When you get benefits through releasing, you'll want to release.

For example, say you have an exam today. If in the past you've had the experience of revising hard and passing, you'll revise hard. But if you pass an exam once through releasing, you'll release. You need to confirm that all your *gain* comes from releasing.

*get everything by releasing only* (getting everything just by releasing). Getting benefits at the beginning is very important. Let *gain* support you in getting higher *gain*.

Put simply: only when you see that the world = suffering and that all happiness comes from within will you turn around and reach Step One. As long as you still think there is happiness in the world, you'll stop to go looking for it.

If you listen to the audios and watch the videos often, you'll notice Lester frequently asks two questions. One is, “Have you experienced changing the *picture* inside and seeing the outside change immediately?” The other is, “Have you discovered that all happiness comes from within?”

Only when you really *discover* this will you achieve Step One. In classical texts like the Āgamas it is expressed as seeing suffering and seeing the arising of suffering. But Lester says it casually, in plain language, so you overlook it.

So turning around really doesn't happen by shouting “I want freedom!” It happens by confirming, bit by bit, that your *gain* comes from releasing. Nothing lies outside the Six Steps.

You can't just resist the world or be disgusted with it — that's no use. Unfortunately, when most people shout “I want freedom!”, what they're suppressing is disgust with the world. But disgust and craving are the same thing: they are two directions of desire.

(**Feng:** Disgust and craving appear together. When you're disgusted with the world, it isn't that you want freedom. You feel the world isn't good enough — you want a better world.)

You have to actually achieve Step One at the level of experience for it to work. Through constant releasing, keep confirming your gains and the source of your happiness. OK — release. From getting a little benefit, to getting every benefit. There's nothing else to say.

### Sharing 2: You keep trying to find happiness in the outer world. Use the goals you want to stir up feelings and release them, and keep confirming the source of happiness, until you experience that the source of all happiness is “the mind quietening down, letting more of the infinite being within show through”. Then you will turn around and achieve Step One (shared 17 November 2020)

**Z:** Feng, releasing over the last couple of days, I've found that the things I want actually bring up a lot of resistance in me, and the things I don't want actually feel like they have a “benefit”. These outer things I want or don't want only matter because I've given them meaning — they're all illusions. What should I do next? Should I keep doing the goals process and so on, to pull out more of the opposing programs?

**Feng:** If there's something you want, go and achieve it. That's what Lester teaches you. The more you get, the more you'll uncover the real source of happiness. When you really achieve Step One, you don't have to think about this question at all. You don't have to think about whether you want freedom or the world; every action and choice you make from then on will naturally tend towards freedom.

But as long as you still keep open the possibility that “there's happiness in the world”, you won't achieve it. You'll unconsciously go looking for happiness somewhere else, and that becomes your goal — and you need to use that goal to pull the programs out.

Only by achieving goals will you discover that the programs have been running you all along, and only then will your direction truly turn around. Step One isn't a slogan; the Six Steps are a perfect map.

You need to confirm what you gain through releasing, until you discover that all happiness comes from one thing — infinite being. You discover, as Lester said, *the world is completely miserable*.

**Z:** But achieving what you want — that wanting is still pointed towards the world. Is that the necessary path for confirming *gain*?

**Feng:** If you suppress it, it stays inside, affecting you, and you'll never achieve Step One. When you achieve a goal, you'll find you didn't get the happiness you imagined. Then you'll stir up the next goal, the next want for the world. When you achieve that, you find you still haven't found the happiness you wanted, so your desire moves on to the next goal after that.

You keep rising, keep confirming the source of happiness, until you experience that the source of all happiness is your “mind quietening down, letting more of the infinite being within show through”. Then your direction turns around. That is Step One completely fulfilled, and freedom becomes your only goal.

After that, as Lester said, you automatically fall into the remaining five steps — that's all. *(laughs)*

**Z:** OK, I get it. If I don't go and achieve it, I'm actually suppressing, and still being run by the three basic wants.
