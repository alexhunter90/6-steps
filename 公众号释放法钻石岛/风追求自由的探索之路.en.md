From the series *Feng on the Six Steps* · Release Method Diamond Island · 29 November 2021

**Feng's Path of Exploration in Search of Freedom**

— Once I experienced how efficient and complete releasing is, I finally dropped every other method

**Shared on 22 November 2020**

**Y:** Feng, you weren't interested when I asked you to write an autobiography. But could you tell us in detail about your experience of learning the Release Method? It would also be a summary of your “path to the cessation of suffering”.

**Feng:** It's hardly a path to the “cessation of suffering”.

**Y:** I think it is. All right — a “path” of releasing, then. What year did you come across it? Why did you want to learn the Release Method back then?

**Feng:** It must have been 2014. Actually I'd seen the film *Letting Go* in 2013, but what I saw then was the Triple Welcome, and I didn't pay it much attention.

**Y:** You'd studied the Buddhadharma so well — why try the Release Method? *(Feng forwards a chat record from 18 November 2020.)*

**Feng:** Meditative absorption felt very good, but to me it felt like a kind of escape. I'd concentrate my attention on one particular object, focus only on that one object, and my troubles would quickly fall away. The body became light and at ease, and the mind became joyful. Coming out of it, there was an indescribable sense of composure. A stable connection was set up between the mind and the object.

With that composure, whatever problem I thought about, the answer would surface quickly. That's how I got through my studies. But when emotions surged up, I sometimes found I couldn't suppress them. Sometimes my surroundings didn't allow me to enter absorption, and I felt dominated by something. That feeling of being pushed along by an inner force hadn't been removed at all — I was only suppressing it.

After I learned the Release Method this became especially obvious, so I gave up practising absorption completely. I knew it wasn't the right direction for me.

**Feng:** That's why I gave up absorption. Giving up insight meditation came later, a while after that.

**Y:** Why did you give up insight meditation?

**Feng:** Because I experienced for myself that the Release Method is more efficient. And it wasn't because I was bad at insight meditation — the senior practitioners all thought my insight practice was already very efficient.

**Y:** Of course insight practice is efficient if you have a foundation in absorption. Did you give up insight meditation after experiencing the Release Method?

**Feng:** Mm. When it comes to practice, I'm a pragmatist.

**Y:** Your insight meditation was so good — why go and try the Release Method? Just curiosity?

**Feng:** With Goenka's Vipassana I felt my progress was a bit slow. I asked the teacher about realising the fruit, and the teacher said, “*don't expect*”.

**L:** Why not expect? How far had that teacher got? Not realised the fruit, I suppose.

**Feng:** Goenka never said he had realised the fruit either. Later I started searching for methods for realising the fruit quickly, and finally I found the Sunlun method. Sunlun is the only one that promises you can realise the fruit within weeks, months or a few years, depending on each person's karma. Sunlun's teaching treats karma as something limiting: even after realising the fruit you're not all-knowing and all-powerful, but still subject to certain limitations.

**Y:** Did you know about the Release Method before you came across the Sunlun method?

**Feng:** I knew about it before, but I wasn't mainly using it then.

**Y:** How long did you practise the Sunlun method? How well did it work? What's the longest you sat?

**Feng:** It worked better than Goenka's method. I could sit for a whole day at a time.

**Y:** Had you tried a bit of the Release Method before practising the Sunlun method?

**Feng:** I'd tried some.

**L:** When did you first try the Release Method — what year and month?

**Feng:** In the summer holidays before the last term of my final year of secondary school.

**Y:** Back to Sunlun — what sticking point did you hit with Sunlun?

**Feng:** It wasn't a sticking point. Sunlun really did make me a lot lighter; I felt it wearing away my karmic habits. But I discovered that one easy release was worth several days of me sitting there suffering. I tested it several times, and it really was so. At first it was hard to accept.

**Y:** So you tried the Release Method while practising Sunlun?

**Feng:** I tried it when I was resting. One release was worth months of Goenka Vipassana, or several days of gruelling Sunlun practice.

**Y:** So over those two months you practised Sunlun and tried the Release Method several times as well, right?

**Feng:** I only tried it a few times at first, then gradually more. Once I released constantly for a whole afternoon, and the effect on how quiet my mind was went far beyond what I'd expected. And it wasn't the surface quiet I used to get from practising absorption. I clearly felt that “the inner volcano” (the subconscious mind) had quietened down too.

**Y:** Did you experience the feeling of releasing then?

**Feng:** I'd experienced the feeling of releasing very early on.

**Y:** Before you came across Sunlun?

**Feng:** Yes — as soon as I started using the Release Method. Back then I even recommended it to some senior practitioners. Some of them were dismissive; some thought it was good. I also recommended it to a few fellow practitioners I'd met while studying Northern-transmission Buddhism, and they used it to help with their Buddha-name recitation. *(laughs heartily)*

**Y:** The summer holidays before the last term of your final year — you mean the summer after the university entrance exams?

**Feng:** No, the summer before the final term of the final year started. Before that I'd only heard of the Release Method; I hadn't really used it.

**Y:** When did you first hear of the Release Method?

**Feng:** The year before, in a Northern-transmission Buddhism group. I saw the film *Letting Go* and also downloaded *The Abundance Book*.

> [Note: *Letting Go* mostly promotes Hale's own version of releasing, not Lester's original Release Method, so we don't recommend watching it. In Larry's *The Abundance Book* and Hale's *The Sedona Method*, the releasing methods have both changed the simple, efficient original method to some degree. This weakens its effect, and they can't take you to ultimate freedom, so these two books can also be set aside.]

**Y:** So you saw the film *Letting Go* in 2013 — that must have been your first encounter with the Release Method.

**Feng:** It was an encounter with Hale, more like, because strictly speaking the Triple Welcome isn't the Release Method. *(laughs heartily)*

**Y:** So 2013 you came across the Release Method, 2014 you practised Vipassana, 2015 Sunlun?

**Feng:** Pa-Auk meditation I'd started back in junior secondary school.

**Y:** Second year? And Daoist internal alchemy in the first year?

**Feng:** The second term of the second year. I'd been studying internal alchemy since primary school. [To keep everyone focused on the Release Method, some of the conversation about internal alchemy is left out here.]

**Feng:** Later I stopped practising the Daoist alchemical path. Buddhism suited my mind better.

**Y:** Did you first attain the first jhāna in the third year of junior secondary?

**Feng:** Around then. After a year or two of practice I reached the second jhāna. Then I stopped practising absorption and spent my time thinking about doctrine, while also coming into contact with other pure-insight methods, like Goenka, Luang Por Pramote and Mahasi. At the same time I also came into contact with Northern-transmission Zen, Tantric Buddhism, Pure Land, Yogācāra and so on.

**Y:** Didn't you try developing supernatural powers? Kids are usually curious about that.

**Feng:** By then my curiosity about supernatural powers had turned into curiosity about the truth. My curiosity about absolute truth outweighed my curiosity about powers.

**Y:** So you started studying philosophy?

**Feng:** I started studying psychology, philosophy, physics… every field to do with explaining the world. [Some conversation about family and other interests is left out here.]

**Y:** You've been going non-stop all the way. You didn't take many detours.

**Feng:** I took quite a few detours, actually.

**Y:** Which ones?

**Feng:** Everything before I found the Release Method was a detour.

**Y:** How have your five or six years of practising the Release Method gone?

**Feng:** From Larry to Hale, with Xu Yaoren's Zero Resistance mixed in. At first I got quite stuck using the “tube” method. Then one time, reading about “decompressing” in *The Abundance Book*, I understood that the feeling itself also wants to get out. So I let go of my effort to hold it down, and it went out. Later I read Hale's *The Sedona Method* and started using the questions. I found the questions extremely effective, so I gave up *The Abundance Book*. But the further I went in Hale's book, the more doubts I had. So I went further and read *The Way to Ultimate Freedom* and *The Ultimate Truth*, and watched two of Lester's videos. On YouTube I heard Lester's later recordings, and finally understood that the Release Method is a complete system. So I wanted to restore the original Release Method. At first I tried to find the parts where Larry and Hale overlapped, but later I discovered someone had uploaded the 1992 course, so I started learning the 1992 Release Method.

**Y:** You watched the 1992 videos last year?

**Feng:** Two years ago.

**Y:** What problems did you run into after watching the 1992 videos?

**Feng:** Getting stuck. No other problems. In theory, Lester's recordings had already given me complete answers. It was just that I often got stuck, and often stopped releasing.

**Y:** Why did you get stuck? Hadn't you already mastered the method? Hadn't you understood the Six Steps correctly? It must have been not understanding Step One and Step Six correctly.

**Feng:** Step One, Step Two and Step Six — I lacked a correct understanding of all of them.

**Y:** What's the problem with Step Two? Isn't it just a decision?

**Feng:** It is the awakening factor of investigation. Truly achieving it means every one of your choices tends towards freedom. Because if you decide on freedom this moment and your next decision heads towards the world, you cancel it out. You have to achieve Step One completely to achieve Step Two completely.

**Y:** Then isn't that really a problem with Step One? If Step One is fine, Step Two is fine. How did you break through Step One?

**Feng:** I achieved Step One by putting the Six Steps into practice correctly. That is, at the start the Six Steps are something you have to operate. Once you achieve Step One, the Six Steps carry you along from then on.

> **Translator's notes**
> - ***Letting Go*** is a film promoting Hale Dwoskin's version of the Sedona Method. The **Triple Welcome** is one of Hale's techniques.
> - **Goenka** is S. N. Goenka, who taught Vipassana. **Sunlun** is a Burmese meditation method (Sunlun Sayadaw). **Pa-Auk** is a Burmese method emphasising absorption (jhāna). **Luang Por Pramote** and **Mahasi** are teachers of insight meditation.
> - **“Northern-transmission Buddhism”** (北传佛法) is Mahayana Buddhism as practised in China.
> - **“Realising the fruit”** (证果) means attaining one of the four stages of awakening, starting with stream-entry.
> - **The awakening factor of investigation** (择法觉支, *dhamma-vicaya*) is one of the seven factors of awakening. Feng links it to Step Two: discerning and choosing rightly, every time.
> - **The “tube” method and “decompressing”** are techniques from Larry Crane's *The Abundance Book*. The Chinese gives no further detail.
> - ***The Way to Ultimate Freedom*** and ***The Ultimate Truth*** are Chinese titles of books of Lester's teachings.
> - The education terms follow the Chinese system: three years of junior secondary school, then three of senior secondary, ending with the university entrance exams.
