## ► Feng tells how he achieved Step One

**20 November 2020**

**Feng:** Releasing can achieve anything. Make that your own experience. During my retreat I also achieved a lot of goals. I didn't use the goal sheets; I just listed the things I wanted to achieve, then kept on releasing, and afterwards found that most of them had come about, so I crossed them off.

**R:** During the retreat, had you already stepped into Step One?

**Feng:** It was on the later occasion that I verified the Six Steps. That time, when I verified the Six Steps, I knew that something had transformed completely. Later I suddenly realised that this was what the Āgama sutras call stream-entry. And after that, reading the Venerable One's explanation, I learned that it is also *nirvikalpa samadhi* in yoga.

Verifying is achieving Step One. Only when you achieve Step One can you cut off doubt, and only once doubt is cut off can it be called “verifying”. The whole path appears before you. You know directly how to go on from here to the end — a direct knowing that doesn't come through thinking. Then you find that none of your actions can go in the direction opposite to freedom any more. This feeling of the flow changing direction is extremely clear.

**L:** How long do you have to release before you know directly?

**Feng:** You mean know the Six Steps directly?

**L:** Mm. How long did you release continuously?

**Feng:** I released for less than a week.

**L:** In retreat, releasing for a whole week?

**Feng:** Mm, releasing constantly. My constant releasing wasn't sitting there blankly, forcing myself to release. I had written lots of goals down. My mental activity was testing.

This is what it was like for me: I'd do a release and feel very good. Then I'd say to myself, “If I release once more, will I feel even better?” Testing Step Six. Then I'd get stuck, and I'd say, “Let's test Step Five. Release the wanting control and see whether it really gets better.” I'd look — ha, sure enough, it was flowing again. So happy. Then release once more.

So the whole process was actually quite fun. It wasn't the grim, ascetic sitting that many people imagine. After releasing constantly for a few days, I already felt very happy, and at that point insights arose naturally. I just knew directly what the only source of happiness is. And when you cover it up with feelings, that is suffering; suffering is limiting your infinite being. The moment this realisation happened, I immediately felt that something had changed. Releasing became very active — it no longer needed any enticement from those goals on paper. I just kept releasing and releasing, and then I reached the point I've talked about: I suddenly pulled free of the whole mind. It felt as though all the force my mind was using turned inward. In that single instant it felt as though my whole body and mind had turned around, and then I suddenly knew all the steps that came after.

At the time I thought those experiences were personal to me, so I didn't describe many of them in detail. For example, when the pressure in my body lifted, I suddenly relaxed completely and sat bolt upright. Now I know this is bound to happen, because it is a precondition for entering samadhi, and also one of the earlier limbs leading to *nirvikalpa samadhi*.

It really is the best thing I've ever experienced: the change in the direction my life flows.

The method is very simple. The Six Steps — practise them for a month. In one to two months, you'll be completely free. If you can't do it in a month or two, then it's impossible. Simple, or impossible. The method is so simple that you don't need an idol at all, and so fast that you don't need a role model, because you don't need to look to anyone else; every release deepens your own experience. This method doesn't need any belief or faith from you at all.

— Feng, 29 December 2020
