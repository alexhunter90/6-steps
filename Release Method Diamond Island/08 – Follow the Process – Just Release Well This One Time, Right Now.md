Original article · from the series *Feng on the Six Steps* · Release Method Diamond Island · 8 January 2022, 12:19

**▲ Topic: Follow the process — just release well this one time, right now**

**Shared on 22 February 2021**

**Feng:** While you're releasing, the mind is what gets in your way: its content, the things you've presupposed — even the idea of how long you think it will take to release something gets in your way. Just do this one release well. There's no need to presuppose that this time you'll release all of it, or that it will take so many times to release all of it. Just release, and see what happens.

Don't rush. Enjoy the process of each release, and keep using the releasing process. The things you think are hard to release — that's the mind telling you they're hard. Ignore the mind and go straight to the process. The things you think are easy or trivial and so overlook — that's the mind telling you they're trivial. Ignore the mind there too, and go straight to the process.

Sometimes you see a feeling and think it isn't the main one. You badly want to release “the main one” and get it over with quickly, so you suppress the feeling that's coming up right now — and this feeling is very important. The mind's judgements that this one or that one is more important have no basis. Whatever is surfacing is what matters. If you suppress it, you're living in the mind. You're using the mind to presuppose what you need to release, including how many times and how long. When you're truly releasing, there's no need for that. Keep open the possibility of releasing all of it in one go.

**Rayna:** What about body sensations?

**Feng:** What do you feel about the body sensations? Do you want to control them? Want to change them? Just release that.

**Rayna:** Got it.

**Feng:** If the body sensations are bothering you, release the being bothered. Use releasing to solve problems.

**Rayna:** OK. I do want them to go — I want to change them.

**Feng:** A few releases, and the whole of a long-standing habit is released — can you imagine that? Very often that's how it is: a few minutes, not a long, bogged-down struggle.

**S:** I'd presupposed it would take a long time, not a few minutes.

**Feng:** In the videos you can see it's a few minutes.

**S:** Sometimes I feel a few minutes is too long; I want to settle it in one go.

**Feng:** If it's taking too long, you're bogged down, you're suppressing, you're not releasing. Then you're suppressing it — trying to keep it from coming up.

**S:** For this suppressing, how should I use the process? Relax first, right?

**Feng:** Relax, then notice the feeling that's most on the surface and ask it directly what it is. Don't wait, dig or look for some special feeling. If you can't make it out, that's wanting control. Don't be especially keen to identify it precisely; just answer by intuition — feeling it is enough.

**S:** OK.

**Feng:** Don't try to keep up the momentum of releasing. Just consciously release this one time, now, and do it well.

**Rayna:** OK. Right now I'm doing it again and again too, because I stop so easily — I can't face those feelings, I can't keep it going.

**Feng:** Don't try to keep it going. Consciously release once, then consciously release once more — that is constancy. It isn't using the mind to insist on a concept of constancy. That “intensive class” XX ran had everyone shouting “release constantly!” every day, all pumped up. In fact they were only releasing on the surface, using the mind to *hold* on to the concept of “constant”. That kind of mental imagining isn't truly being constant; it's just trying to control.

You can't be *pushed* to freedom. Freedom is a choice of free will; it can't come from pressure in your circumstances, because your circumstances are created by your mind. Releasing this one time, now, doesn't need any forcing. And then you keep choosing to release — that is the decision.

Shouting slogans is no use; it stays in the mind. Shouting slogans with a crowd is even less use — that's collective unconsciousness. Like the ant colonies I've watched: collective, unconscious action. Self-reliance isn't being different in how you behave; it's recognising your full responsibility. Recognising that whether you are free or not, only you can decide.

The mind is familiar to you, so you stay in the mind. Don't turn “releasing constantly” into a mental activity. Because the mind is what you know, it's very easy for you to turn releasing into a mental activity too. Every release brings something new, and every release broadens your mind. Don't meet it with a habitual attitude.

Treat every release as your first release, and see what happens. Treat every feeling as nothing more than a feeling, and face it as that. Don't add drama. And if outer confirmation shows that nothing has changed, then straight away you have a feeling you can release.

**Feng:** Follow the process — just release well this one time, right now. Releasing constantly is an experience. Don't try to avoid the feelings that aren't obvious; all feelings are the same.

**G:** I always forget. In my head, feelings are divided into ones that are easy to release, ones that are hard to release, ones that can be released and ones that can't — all ranked into classes.

**Feng:** Don't listen to it. *(laughs)*

**L:** If I don't break through soon I'll go mad. If this is the first light of dawn, how much longer until it's day?

**Feng:** Then it will never be day, because you're thinking about the future. Or — release once, right now, and it's day.

**L:** *(laughs)* All right.

**Feng:** The word “persevere” means you're clinging tightly to something, instead of being aware of it now and releasing it. That attitude to action isn't quite right. “Believe there's light ahead and keep going” — you'd rather escape into feel-good platitudes than release this wanting to change. When you release, the whole mind's views and ideas change at once, and the mind's experience broadens. To be aware of it and not release it, but hold on to it hard — that's too silly. Post a couple of platitudes, others encourage you a bit, and you *hold* on even harder. *(laughs)*

**L:** *(three emojis)*

**Feng:** Keeping it simple really is too hard for you lot. *(laughs)* Keep it simple, keep it simple. Needing to “persevere” shows your releasing isn't easy and natural, and releasing that isn't easy and natural shows you're suppressing. It's very simple logic.

Suppress, and you'll never see the dawn.

Release, and the dawn is here now.

Just release. Just go through the process once, right now, and you're immediately lighter and happier, and the problem becomes lighter too. Every change happens in this moment. Don't wait for the future: the future = never comes.

What are you waiting for?

Isn't that wanting to change (wanting control)?

You're still holding on tight and not releasing — are you counting on things changing in the future by “waiting”? Only if you release it now will anything change. Can a “waiting process” release suppressed feelings?

**L:** No. *(laughs)*

**Feng:** Only the “releasing process” can do it. Whatever you think will come about in the future, the mind will keep it in the future for ever.
