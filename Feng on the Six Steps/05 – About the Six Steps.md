# About the Six Steps

---

In his book, Hale introduces the Six Steps like this:

> “The Golden Six Steps are a highly condensed summary of the Sedona Method. Lester created the concept of the ‘Golden Six Steps’ in 1974 to sum up the whole process of releasing. At the time he was working on the theory of the Sedona Method with a small group of people. While training them, he worked with them to systematise the teaching of the Sedona Method, and along the way explored ways of teaching yourself the method.
>
> The concept of the Golden Six Steps suddenly came to him, and he immediately wrote it on the bookmark in the book he was holding. Since it was created, the concept hasn't changed much — it is classic and perfect.
>
> Following the Golden Six Steps as you practise releasing is very useful. Many people write the six steps on a slip of paper and carry it in their wallet; some even write them on their daily to-do list. You could also stick them on the wall in front of your desk, or make them your screensaver. In short, any way that reminds you to release, any time and anywhere, will do.
>
> I also suggest that when you practise releasing intensively, you keep a copy of the Golden Six Steps in front of you, so you can rescue yourself when you get stuck. As I said, these six steps represent the most central, essential part of all the exercises you have done or not done.”

> **Translator's note:** This is translated from the Chinese edition of Hale Dwoskin's *The Sedona Method*. His original English isn't in the repository, so the wording won't match his exactly.

**[17 September 2020] Feng:** As Lester put it, all the knowledge the Release Method needs is in the Six Steps. You just need a simple, direct path, and then to walk it without looking back.

So anything that encourages you to keep releasing, you can look at. Anything that makes you stop releasing, you should be wary of. As soon as I stop releasing, I know I've gone wrong — at least, I'm not on the path. Something that stops you progressing is no use, however holy its packaging.

That's roughly my view. Just practise simply and the truth will reveal itself to you; there's no need to dress it up in words on this shore. As long as you haven't experienced it, you can switch between countless ways of describing it, and it still won't be yours.

---

**[30 September 2020] Feng:** Lester liked to put difficult concepts in the simplest possible language, so in translating I also try to keep it everyday and avoid big words. I can't even find the original version of the Six Steps. Lester never gave a single, focused talk on the Six Steps. He mentioned them here and there, or just referred to the *six step*, but if you piece it together you get more or less the whole thing. Let me try translating them afresh, using his own words.

**Step One:** You must want freedom more than you want the world. (You must want freedom more than you want approval and control. Approval and control = the world.)

**Step Two:** Make the decision to be free.

**Step Three:** All feelings come from wanting control and wanting approval. They are survival programs. Release them.

**Step Four:** Release constantly.

**Step Five:** When you get stuck, release the wanting to change the stuck feeling.

**Step Six:** Each time you release, you are happier, lighter and freer of limitation. As you go on releasing, you become happier and happier, lighter and lighter, and freer and freer of limitation.

There — these are the Six Steps, pieced together from Mr Lester's scattered words, for reference.

---

**[30 September 2020] Feng:** As for knowledge, all we need to know is the Six Steps and the releasing process. The rest is just doing it and experiencing it, over and over. If you disagree about something conceptual, put it aside for now and go and actually release.
