Translation into Chinese: Feng · From the recording *Lester on the Mind*

Anything you develop in the mind will stop you from getting to the top. The mind is your greatest enemy.

All this misery has no meaning whatsoever. I've had enough of it, and I've ended it. I'll put everything into staying in this state.

It will take a month, maybe two, and you'll release all your *agflap*.

But all in all, I think we have the only method for releasing the mind — one that can bring the mind to stillness by bringing the subconscious programs out into view.

We start by going into the conscious mind and releasing control and approval. Take the other things you try to release, like fear — you know, you could spend hundreds or thousands of lifetimes releasing fear, and after that you'd still have anger, you'd still have other emotions.

But when you release wanting control, everything underneath it is let go. And then when you release wanting security, you release all the wanting approval and control, and everything beneath them.

Right now you're too low to launch an attack on wanting security. You're overwhelmed, you're scared to death of death, so you let it run you. But when you've released a fair amount of control and approval, you'll be able to face the fear of death.

But you can see that what our method actually does is release the drives of the mind. Don't try to develop your mind. If you do, you're developing your enemy — developing the very thing that binds you.

What happens when a mind becomes completely quiet? *well*, I have always been all-knowing; I've always known everything.

But you need to release your habit of trying to survive twenty-four hours a day, which we call *agflap*. When you let all the *agflap* go, the body is still there and the world doesn't disappear, but it's like a mirage.

You walk towards it and discover it's only desert. You let the body do whatever it was doing before. But you don't disappear. You're far too afraid of losing your bondage.

Everyone combines releasing with other things, and when you do that, it slows releasing down. If you need other things, you slow down with the method.

You don't need to go chasing after your feelings once you've felt them. They're with you around the clock, and around the clock you're running away from them.

The first item is survival. There's nothing more foolish than working for a living. You are eternal.

Let me tell you straight: becoming number one in the entertainment business is trying to become the strongest ego. And then you see what happens to them. How happy are Burt Reynolds and Glenn Ford? I can only talk about my own generation; I don't know who's popular now.

How happy are they? Do you know them? Have you talked with them? They're more miserable than a labourer. They take drugs; even though they know they shouldn't, they get caught up in it. They drink; they get caught up in it. Some of them killed themselves.

They were without doubt people who had gained fame and fortune, and then they killed themselves. Some did it later on. Judy Garland did it later.

*well*, who doesn't die? Where will the new prince be in a hundred years? Turning circles in front of the public? *(laughter)*

Unfortunately — and you may need to be very careful about this — the most successful people don't go for freedom. The rich people I've talked with could feel, around me, a security they didn't have.

They told me they wanted it too. But when it came to coming to a course? They couldn't come to a course. They looked at me and looked down on me. They had made millions. I wanted them to think I was poor, because it was precisely having millions that kept them stuck — especially the wives of the rich.

I was once invited to high-society parties in New York, to get together with them there. They asked me, “Can you help us?” I said, “Yes, but you have to know that money isn't everything.” At that point the curtain would come down. They'd leave and drop me — and stop wasting my time.

But these rich people have this kind of mind: “It's easy for me; I'm successful.” Of course, if you can use it consciously to show that you are master of the world, that's what I recommend.

When you become master of the world — when you have all that praise, like our stars — you can't stop there. You have to set out to master your mind. See: first you master the world, then you master the mind, and then you're a master.

What is mastering the mind? Getting rid of your *agflap* — that's all it is. Then you truly control the mind. You can play any feeling, and any action you choose is free of all worry.

Once you're free of your *agflap*, you can play any emotion far better than you could when you had *agflap*. When you're in the world, you can use them to communicate.

If someone is furious and you say, “*oh*, it's a wonderful world, everything is so beautiful!”, they won't take in a single word. If you start saying, “*oh*, yes, *blablablabla*”, they'll listen to you.

Once you're free, you'll play — act out feelings — in order to communicate with others. You have to be at their level for them to be able to communicate with you.

So sometimes you'll still use feelings to communicate. The difference is that the feelings no longer run you; you run the feelings. Right now, though, the feelings are running you.

> **Translator's notes**
> - ***agflap*** is the Sedona Method's acronym for Apathy, Grief, Fear, Lust, Anger, Pride.
> - **Burt Reynolds, Glenn Ford and Judy Garland** are American film stars Lester mentions as examples. Judy Garland died in 1969 of an accidental overdose. Lester's point is that fame and fortune did not bring these stars happiness.
