The Shortcut to Freedom · posted 3 March 2021, 08:43
Date of talk: 18 October 2020

**Feng:**
I have decided to go free. I'm going to do it. Yesterday, while I was releasing, this decision became completely clear. The state I'm in now is one my former self wouldn't have dared to imagine, but I know it isn't the end yet.

More than seventy or eighty per cent of the time I'm operating at the level of “knowing”. Simply knowing, without calling up any “knowledge” or “beliefs”. The mind is running far, far less than before.

I have seen what Lester meant when he said “all happiness comes from within”. When we try to get happiness from the world, we create a lack, and that lack drives a great many feelings and thoughts.

When we supposedly get happiness from the world, all that is really happening is that, by telling ourselves “I've achieved my goal”, we temporarily quieten the mind.

A quiet mind = happiness. Because the only source of happiness is beingness, and the mind is the only thing covering up our infinite being.

Go and do it. I know now: freedom can be reached in a few months, or a few weeks. This time it isn't “I believe”, it's “I know” — I've seen the whole picture clearly.

Follow the Six Steps all the way through, and you can do it. Allow yourself to become aware of it, and let it go.

> **Translator's note:** The long passage below is a talk by Lester Levenson. The repository doesn't contain his original English for it, so this is translated from the Chinese. It is close in meaning but is not his exact words.

“**Lester:** Our basic philosophy is very simple: each one of us is unlimited, except for the limitations we hold on to mentally.

When you let go of the mental constraints and limitations, we discover that we have always been unlimited. We have always been all-powerful, all-knowing and perfect, because our basic nature is perfect, and there is nothing we can do to change that. Except that we keep turning our heads and looking elsewhere — ‘it's over there, it's over there’, ‘who am I, who am I’ — like a hopeless drunk, trying to find out who and what we are.

And then a part of us — we call it the mind — blinds us, so that we can't see that our unlimited being is inherent and natural. Twenty-four hours a day, the mind is unconsciously searching the outer world for absolute security, and it isn't there. So every day, every one of us unconsciously spends twenty-four hours going in the wrong direction, looking for where we are, who I am, what we are, and where our security is. It is a one hundred per cent hopeless direction, full of frustration.

If your basic nature is whole, complete and eternal, why do you spend so much of every day struggling to survive? The polite word for it is ignorance. But what I'll actually say to you is that any intelligent person would say, ‘My goodness, if I knew that, I'd become it right away.’

It takes no time to be what you are. The effort needed to be what you are is zero. But being what you are not — being limited — takes endless, laborious struggle, unhappiness and frustration.

Now, all of you have heard what I've said. How many intelligent people are there in this room who will drop the struggle and simply allow their infinite being to be? If you do, you'll have an effortless, extremely joyful life every moment. How many of us have done it? You would rather be miserable; you would rather do what are, in my eyes, the stupidest things. If you can have anything just by a thought, what is all this struggle for?

I'm calling you stupid because it might help you. Is there anyone here who doesn't want to be intelligent? So be intelligent! If you need the world, work it with effortless thought, allow it to fall into your lap, and let go of all your misery.

It's simple, it's easy, it's quick — if you do it. If you don't, it's complicated, extremely hard to reach, frustrating and painful.

But the choice is yours. You can be what you are right now — whole, complete, perfect — or you can choose to look elsewhere, twenty-four hours a day. So what will we do, over the next month or two? Choose to be intelligent, or choose to be stupid?

I'll give you the answer, and I hope I'm wrong: I think you'll choose to be stupid. But I can't work out why you choose to be stupid. Because when I was searching for the answer, the first thing I realised was that I was stupid. I said it more than once. I said, ‘Lester, you are so stupid! Stupid! Stupid! Stupid!’ — three times. And I meant it.

So you hear me talk about stupidity: I did not want to be stupid, and that was the key to my turning around. The next thing, of course, was to look in the right direction. I realised that what I had spent forty years trying to find out there wasn't out there. It was at the very bottom.

So there was only one direction left: inward. What am I? What is the world? What is my relationship to it? What is all this about? Once I asked, the answers came.

After it was all over, I could see the steps I had taken. I packaged them into this method. It is a very efficient technique, but you use it only partially, minimally, not fully and to the maximum. If you used it to the maximum, it would take you only a few months, and then you would have no more suppressed feelings.

The only thing standing between you and your ultimate beingness is your suppressed feelings. So, as is only right, make the decision: stop being stupid, become intelligent, and release all these suppressed feelings — within a month or two.

I don't think I can give you any better revelation than that. I could say you can do it in a century or two — but why not now? You are infinite being right now. Why not simply let them go? Release all the coverings; they are all in your feelings.

Any questions about what I've said? I've said what I had to say.”

You can read this passage again and again to inspire yourself.

**Student:**
Should we wait until we've finished releasing the two big wants — wanting approval and wanting control — and the wanting to survive comes up naturally, before working on that?

**Feng:**
Whenever you can become aware of it, release it. If you can't become aware of it, your “wanting to become aware” is wanting control. Release that, and you'll be able to become aware.

Wanting approval is the cause of wanting control, and wanting to survive is the cause of wanting approval. As you go on releasing, eventually the wanting to survive (the fear of death) becomes quite obvious. That is what I'm mainly releasing now. The wanting control and wanting approval that sit on top of it have been cleared out until there's very little left.

When I say “eventually”, does it sound like something very far off? Actually it's only a week or two of constant releasing. Don't stretch it out into units of “years”, because it doesn't need that.

I'm going into retreat to release. This time I have only one goal, and that is freedom.

---

**Student:**
Why couldn't I release yesterday when I was releasing wanting control and wanting to change? It felt very hard to do this release on its own.

**Feng:**
I'll answer this last question. It's actually very simple. Keep the Six Steps firmly in mind. Keep them close by, where you can pick them up and look at them at any time.

You habitually use control to get things done, and that habit was running you even while you were releasing. That's because you overlooked Step Six: “Each time you release, you become lighter, happier and freer.” You were doing it with *effort*. That isn't right.

**Student:**
Yes, I was releasing with a purpose. Every time, I release really forcefully.

**Feng:**
You've misunderstood. Having a “purpose” isn't the problem; not following the Six Steps is the problem. Let me explain a little further.

When the process says “allow yourself to become aware”, it doesn't mean digging forcefully. It means whatever you become aware of while doing nothing at all, right now. There's no need to summon effort or to use control. Why doesn't the course teach you “how to become aware”? Because “how” contains a mental effort.

For example, ask yourself whether, right now, you want control or want approval. You'll be aware of it straight away. If you aren't, then you want to make yourself be aware. Wanting things to go your way is wanting control. It's very simple.

So, I'll say it again: keep the Six Steps firmly in mind. Keep them close by and pick them up and look at them at any time. Any problem with releasing can be answered by the Six Steps.

Let me go through them one by one.

Step One is “You must want freedom more than you want control and approval.” Wanting control and approval are feelings that limit you. But sometimes we are just that stupid: we would rather have limitation than freedom. Sometimes you get stuck simply because you don't want to release them. *Just want it.* When you recognise that wanting control and approval limit you, you want freedom more. So the Six Steps really need no explanation. They mean exactly what they say.

Step Two: make the decision to be free. A decision is a decision. Because you want freedom, you decide to do it.

Step Three: all feelings are wanting control and wanting approval. You have feelings at every moment. The whole world you see is wanting control and wanting approval. Twenty-four hours a day you are on guard, keeping the fear of death from coming up. The defence mechanism is wanting control and wanting approval.

Step Four: release constantly. Constantly means constantly. You do it once, then you can do it a second time, a third, a fourth… until the last time, when you reach freedom. Lester said, *“there is no stop in the six step”*. There is no “stop” anywhere in the Six Steps. Constantly means constantly.

Step Five is the one you may use most at the beginning, because you'll often get stuck. But being stuck is only wanting control. The only reason you get stuck is that wanting control is operating. It's that simple. Release the wanting control that is running you, and the stuck feeling immediately turns into flow.

Step Six is also the step we most easily overlook. Each release makes you freer. Of course it's also described as lighter and happier, but in the end it all comes down to freedom. The momentum these experiences bring strengthens your wanting freedom in Step One. And Lester said, “If you fully do Step One, you will naturally carry out the other five steps.” If you fully do Step One, nothing stands in your way.

OK — the Six Steps are the Six Steps. Keep them close by, and they will greatly speed up your releasing.

---

**Student:**
Getting what you want by releasing — isn't that control?

**Feng:**
Control is a good thing; wanting control is not. Wanting = lack. Wanting control is a feeling of lacking control.

---

**Student:**
Each time we release, do we need to go through the Six Steps again, including Steps One and Two? Or is the releasing process enough?

**Feng:**
The releasing process is all you need. If you're not releasing, use the Six Steps to remind yourself. If you are releasing, keep on releasing. I'm off to release constantly. Keep going!

> - “Releasing process” (释放流程) refers to the step-by-step releasing procedure taught in the Sedona Method course: allowing yourself to become aware of a feeling, seeing which want it comes from, and letting it go.
> - “Going into retreat” (闭关) means withdrawing from ordinary activity for a period of intensive practice.
